# Parity: Rendering on Lavapipe

* **Scope**: how Mesa's software Vulkan driver, lavapipe, renders our frames differently from a GPU, and what the renderer does about it. Cloud sessions ([ADR 0039](../../docs/adr/0039-cloud-sessions-fetch-pinned-game-archives.md)) and CI ([ADR 0006](../../docs/adr/0006-ci-renders-on-lavapipe.md)) both render on it.
* **Sources**: Mesa 25.2.8 source (`src/gallium/auxiliary/draw/draw_pipe_clip.c`, `src/gallium/drivers/llvmpipe/lp_context.c`), parity runs logged in [`log.md`](log.md) on 2026-11-07, and the GPU control at `aa76d1e` ([`method.md`](method.md) section 3).
* **Evidence tier**: stated at the start of each section.

## 1. Near ground polygons drew palette index 0

`[Bounded Observation]`, 2026-11-07, on `9f04164`; fixed by a guard in `fragment_index`. Rendered on Mesa lavapipe (a cloud session with no GPU; CI's adapter too, [ADR 0006](../../docs/adr/0006-ci-renders-on-lavapipe.md)), CTRY and INTRO scored 0.6446 and 0.6040 against the GPU rows' 0.8725 and 0.8273, with IoU identical. The loss was in the props class: in 52 of 114 CTRY and 69 of 209 INTRO scored frames, large ground decals close to the eye (the field under the hero at CTRY 389-444, the sand at INTRO 1295-1506 and 2632-2786) drew palette index 0 over their whole area, while the same props far away drew their own index (CTRY 1257). `fragment_index` in `mode13h.wgsl` rounds the `@interpolate(linear) shade` varying, and that varying arrived as NaN, which `u32(clamp(floor(NaN + 0.5), 0, 255))` turns into 0. Ruled out at CTRY 422: the actor shadow draw (black with it off) and the decal depth bias (black with slope 0); also ruled out: crossing the near plane as the reason here (section 2). `fragment_index` now falls back to the flat `palette_index` when `shade` is not finite (candidate (c), section 3), and the GPU test `a_ground_polygon_under_a_low_eye_keeps_its_index` fails on lavapipe without it (25,866 of 51,840 ground pixels at index 0). Capture, then lavapipe on `9f04164`, then with the guard: [`ctry-422-lavapipe-nan-shade.png`](../images/2026-11-07-72028d1/ctry-422-lavapipe-nan-shade.png), [`intro-1495-lavapipe-nan-shade.png`](../images/2026-11-07-72028d1/intro-1495-lavapipe-nan-shade.png) ([`log.md`](log.md), 2026-11-07).

## 2. Mechanism: the clipper's screen-space weight is 0/0

`[Supported Inference]`: Mesa source read, its clipper replayed on the frame's own triangles, and a live NaN trap agree; no trap inside Mesa itself. Mesa 25.2.8 (Ubuntu `25.2.8-0ubuntu0.24.04.4`) llvmpipe, under lavapipe, clips in software through the gallium `draw` module and turns the guard band off (`lp_context.c` line 339), so a triangle is clipped against x = -w, x = w, y = -w, y = w, then the half-z near plane, in that order (`draw_pipe_clip.c` `do_clip_tri`). For each new vertex, `interp()` (`draw_pipe_clip.c` lines 183-209) interpolates perspective attributes with the 3D weight `t`, but `noperspective` ones with a screen-space weight `t_nopersp = (dst_x/dst_w - out_x/out_w) / (in_x/in_w - out_x/out_w)`. It picks the axis by testing the clip-space x of the endpoints for inequality, then divides by their screen-space difference. An edge that an earlier pass left lying on a side plane x = ±w has both endpoints at screen x ±1, so the divisor is 0. If that edge reaches behind the eye, the opposite x plane cuts it at w = 0, x = 0, and `dst_x/dst_w` is 0/0. Either way `t_nopersp` is not finite, and `LINTERP(t, a, a) = a + t * 0` is NaN even for an attribute that is the same at every vertex. The near-plane pass then interpolates from that vertex, and the post-clip polygon is emitted as a fan from its first vertex, so every fan triangle carries a NaN vertex and the polygon's whole visible area reads NaN. That is the case observed here, a large ground polygon under a low camera, but not the only one: the divisor is 0 for any edge whose endpoints share a screen x while their clip-space x differ, so the near plane alone can produce it, for example on the edge from (1, -1, -1, 2) to (2, 1, 1, 4) (screen x 0.5 at both ends and at the cut; review on #264). The guard catches every non-finite `shade`, whichever edge produced it. A GPU clips only near and far, with a guard band for x and y. Evidence: (1) a temporary sentinel in `fragment_index` (NaN 250, infinity 251, out of range 252/253, more than 0.5 from `palette_index` 254) marked every layer-3 sample in rows 120-184 at CTRY 422 (10,134) and 400 (9,791) as NaN, and none at 1257. (2) A float32 port of `do_clip_tri`/`interp` run on the 2,203 world-prop triangles within 1,500 m of the eye, with the tick's dumped `view_proj`: 184 need clipping and 62 of them cross the near plane, yet exactly one gives NaN. That one is the index-196 triangle of the 128 m field tile under the eye (x 3200-3328, z 2432-2560). Its first NaN comes where an edge on x = w (both endpoints at screen x 1.0) is cut by the opposite x plane at w = 0, and its clipped quad has 2 NaN vertices. The other 61 near-crossing triangles stay finite, so the near-plane clip alone is not the cause in this frame. Related upstream history: Mesa bug 98851 (2016, `noperspective` attributes wrongly interpolated after clipping) concerns the same `t_nopersp` code; no report of this zero divisor was found.

## 3. Candidate fixes

Lavapipe only; a `DBG_SHADE` sweep plus one `mise run parity` per candidate, on `b0c90c2` and `9f04164` (identical renderer); (c) adopted. `mean_ssim_scored` CTRY / INTRO, IoU 0.9897 / 0.9856 in every row, then index agreement:

* base (`linear`, rounded): 0.6446 / 0.6040; 0.663 / 0.612.
* (a) `shade` `@interpolate(perspective)`: 0.8722 / 0.8281; 0.8893 / 0.8289. Not byte-identical on a GPU: a Gouraud (`GVPOLY`) polygon's index becomes perspective-correct instead of screen-linear, which departs from PLAYONE's filler ([`terrain-shade.md`](../playone-exe/terrain-shade.md) section 6). Its lavapipe stores differ from (c)'s.
* (b) flat `palette_index` unless the polygon is Gouraud, marked by a temporary vertex flag bit 3 set in `tessellate.rs` when `vertex_palette` is non-empty: 0.8722 / 0.8281; 0.8893 / 0.8289. On a GPU it should be byte-identical: flat polygons already round to their own index, and Gouraud ones keep `linear`. It needs a `SodVertex` flag (a seam-contract change), and Gouraud polygons stay exposed on lavapipe: its INTRO store differs from (c)'s, its CTRY store does not.
* (c) guard, adopted: a non-finite `shade` (exponent bits all set) falls back to the flat `palette_index`: 0.8722 / 0.8281; 0.8893 / 0.8289. On a GPU it should be byte-identical, since a GPU's linear interpolation of finite vertex values is finite and the branch never fires. Shader-only. On lavapipe a Gouraud polygon hit by the bug draws its provoking vertex's index instead of its gradient. A divisor that is one ulp rather than zero gives a huge but finite weight, which would mis-shade a Gouraud polygon unguarded (not observed).
* (b) and (c) together: byte-identical to (c) on lavapipe on both films.
* flat `palette_index` everywhere (diagnostic, drops `GVPOLY` interpolation): 0.8721 / 0.8231; 0.8895 / 0.8282.

## Ruled out

* **The actor shadow draw** (CTRY 422): with it off, the ground stays black.
* **The decal depth bias** (CTRY 422): with slope 0, the ground stays black.
* **Crossing the near plane as the cause in this frame**: 61 of 62 near-crossing triangles at CTRY 422 stay finite (section 2), though an edge of constant screen x can produce the NaN at the near plane alone.
* **A renderer change, #249's adapter choice included, as the cause of the lavapipe loss**: guarding the one NaN value leaves lavapipe less than the 0.002 adoption margin from the GPU at the same renderer (section 3), too little for a renderer change to hide a 0.23 loss behind.

## Open questions

1. Whether (c) leaves GPU output byte-identical: it should, since the branch fires only on a non-finite `shade`, but no GPU has rendered it yet; a GPU `mise run parity` must match the rows before it. With (c), lavapipe scores CTRY 0.8722 and INTRO 0.8281 against the RTX 4070's 0.8725 and 0.8273 at `aa76d1e`, the same renderer: -0.0003 and +0.0008, below the 0.002 adoption margin, which is a policy bound rather than a noise model ([`method.md`](method.md) section 3). Lavapipe rows may stand for GPU rows once a GPU run with the guard agrees. On lavapipe a Gouraud polygon the clipper breaks still draws one flat index, and a near-zero but finite weight would mis-shade it unguarded (not observed). The hero's shadow at CTRY 422 is still drawn in patches on lavapipe, where the capture shows it solid; unattributed. ([#265](https://github.com/neilsh/stunt-reel/issues/265))
2. Whether Mesa after 25.2.8 changed `t_nopersp` in `draw_pipe_clip.c`; not filed upstream. Reading it needs `gitlab.freedesktop.org`, which cloud sessions cannot reach by default ([#265](https://github.com/neilsh/stunt-reel/issues/265)).
