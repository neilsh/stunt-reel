# Parity: World Draws on Lavapipe

* **Scope**: what the static world's base and decal draws cost a headless render on lavapipe, Mesa's software Vulkan driver, where lavapipe spends that time, and how much of the world a frame's walk emits; split from [`lavapipe-render-time.md`](lavapipe-render-time.md), which holds which renderer changes cut render time and the loop's other phases, with the spans, machines and Mesa builds these figures name.
  How a render's memory divides, the world's device buffers before #1093 removed them included, is in [`lavapipe-render-workers.md`](lavapipe-render-workers.md).
* **Sources**: timed renders, stack samples and CPU counts from #622 to #1090; the spans (A `LOCATION 12..=300`, B `LOCATION 3680..=3979`, the whole `LOCATION 12..=8262`), machines and Mesa builds are defined in [`lavapipe-render-time.md`](lavapipe-render-time.md).
* **Evidence tier**: `[Bounded Observation]` throughout unless a claim says otherwise; each figure holds for the tree, machine and span it names.

## 1. What the world's base and decal draws cost

**The world's base and decal draws on lavapipe (#920, `67f409b7`).**
Skipping all four kinds (base and decal, fill and outline) cut the whole `LOCATION` film's lavapipe loop 49.0% (27,986 to 14,272.5 ms, on `dc9622ea`) and Span B's 70.7% to 72.9%; on the RTX 4070 the whole film's fell 5.8% and Span B's 20.6% to 26.8%.
On Span B the decal fills alone cut 42.9% and the decal outlines alone 47.4%, not additive: encoding each world draw's decal fills together and then its outlines, every draw and pixel kept, cut 40.2%, which puts the fill-to-outline pipeline switch at about 55% of what the four skips save there (attributed by elimination).
A 1-pixel scissor on every world base and decal draw cut Span B only 11.8%, the base outlines alone 11.1%, the base fills alone 5.3% (unresolved).
Over the whole film (2 rounds) outlines and pixels weigh more: base outlines `-23.1%`, the scissor `-26.1%`, the decal grouping `-7.7%`, all four `-48.0%`.
The draws skipped carry a median 4,423 vertices a frame on Span B (1,000 over the whole film), so their cost is not fill-vertex work (their outlines were not counted).
On Span A the resident set's 172.6 MiB rise during rendering does not move with these draws.
_Source: [the world base and decal draws](../../work/2026-11/distill-lavapipe-render-time/journal.md#2026-11-22-where-the-world-base-and-decal-draws-cost-sits-920), [verdicts](../../work/2026-11/distill-lavapipe-render-time/journal.md#2026-11-22-optimization-verdicts-as-of-f6976e67)._

**The plane shader's `prop_shape` lookup (#920, `a0fcd348`).**
Replacing its binary search of the world's prop runs (31,441 on `LOCATION`) with a constant shape word cut the lavapipe loop 5.9% on Span B and 10.8% over the whole film (about 0.35 ms a frame), faster in 4 of 4 pairs, and moved nothing resolved on RTX 4070 Span B; the shapes store differed, so it is a probe, and 10.8% bounds what a per-vertex `shape_id` could save, from above.
The per-vertex shape word that replaced the lookup (#1001) cut the whole film's loop 8.3% (one run of 4 pairs, faster in 3) and Span B's 5.9% with stores unchanged, and slowed the RTX 4070's whole film 4.6% ([`lavapipe-render-time.md` § 1](lavapipe-render-time.md#1-changes-that-cut-render-time)).
_Source: [the per-fragment `prop_shape` lookup](../../work/2026-11/distill-lavapipe-render-time/journal.md#2026-11-22-what-the-per-fragment-prop_shape-lookup-costs-920)._

**Decal pipeline switches batching would leave (#1000, `7bfc6cca`, CPU count).**
Each world decal slice switches pipelines twice a frame (fill, then outline), or once with no outline.
Greedy batches of consecutive slices whose node boxes, projected to 320×200 and grown 1 pixel, are pairwise disjoint would leave 228,160 of the whole `LOCATION` film's 421,326 switches (54.2%, 1.85 slices a batch) and 53,728 of Span B's 117,228 (45.8%, 2.18 a batch); with the boxes not grown, 43.6% and 29.4%, a sensitivity run.
_Source: [decal pipeline switches](../../work/2026-11/distill-lavapipe-render-time/journal.md#2026-11-22-decal-pipeline-switches-batching-would-leave-1000)._

## 2. Where lavapipe spends the whole `LOCATION` loop

**Where lavapipe spends the loop on the whole `LOCATION` film (`#1002`, `f6976e67`).**
`[Bounded Observation]`, 2026-11-23, Windows `mesa-dist-win 25.2.8`, a user-mode stack sampler at about 190 Hz with mesa-dist-win's `vulkan_lvp.pdb`, two sampled runs a cell on the whole film and Span B (one for Span B's skip-all), the sampler adding `1.9%..6.9%` to the loop and leaving each variant's stores byte-identical to its unsampled runs'.
The main thread waits on lavapipe's queue fence for `59.3%` of the loop, so lavapipe's queue thread, which executes every command buffer and is busy or waiting on the rasterizers for `96.5%..96.8%` of the loop, is its critical path; a part's share of that thread's samples is its share of the loop while the main thread waits, and the main thread's own work (`37.3%`, `wgpu` included) would cap what any part saves, if that work does not change.
The queue thread's parts are the rasterizers' work it waits for (`26.7%`: about `78%` of their busy samples JIT fragment shading, which has no symbols to split between `fs_main_plane`, `prop_shape` and the rest, and primitives with 4 edge planes, lines among them, `7.6%..8.2%`; `3.5%..3.7%` have no Mesa owner), the draw module's vertex work (`21.4%`), line setup and binning (`14.6%`), the fragment-state store (`10.2%`: llvmpipe's `try_update_scene_state`, whose `memcpy` and `memcmp` take `9.2%`), other state changes and pipeline binds (`8.5%`) and triangle setup and binning (`5.4%`, `0.4` points above the 5% bar, less than the sampler's cost to the loop, so it may not clear the bar unsampled); the queue submit's own overhead is `2.1%` there and `1.5%` on the main thread.
The world's part (base minus `#920`'s skip-all) is `13.0%` of the loop for lines, `11.0%` vertex, `7.5%` rasterization, `4.5%` the state store, `4.2%` other state and `2.3%` triangles: mostly line setup, vertex work and rasterization (`9,102` of `13,620 ms`).
Each leans low, by about 2 points of the base loop in total: the sampler costs the base loop `+1.9%` and the skip-all `+6.1%`, so the sampled loops cut `47.1%` where the unsampled ones cut `49.2%`.
On Span B the state store is the largest single part of the loop (`28.3%`, `745 ms`, `5 ms` with skip-all; `740` of the world's `1,948.5 ms` there, about `38%`), and llvmpipe's first-use shader compiles take a further `17.2%`, all in the loop's first half second, consistent with `#920`'s grouping probe saving far more on Span B than on the whole film.
`[Supported Inference]` from Mesa 25.2.8's source and the PDB: the store compares the current fragment state with the one last stored and, when they differ, copies a `32,440`-byte `lp_rast_state` into the scene (its `lp_jit_resources` arrays are `32,384` bytes); the stores a frame are not counted.
_Source: [where Windows lavapipe spends the loop on the whole LOCATION film](../../work/2026-11/1002_lavapipe-world-profile/journal.md#2026-11-23-where-windows-lavapipe-spends-the-loop-on-the-whole-location-film), sampler [`stack_sampler.py`](../../work/2026-11/1002_lavapipe-world-profile/stack_sampler.py), patch [`profile-probe.patch`](../../work/2026-11/1002_lavapipe-world-profile/profile-probe.patch) (`#920`'s loop timer and skip-all switch), [as this note stated it](../../work/2026-11/distill-lavapipe-render-time-3/journal.md#2026-11-23-where-lavapipe-spends-the-loop-on-the-whole-location-film-as--3-stated-it-1002)._

## 3. What one frame emits and the shared slices

**How much of the world one frame's walk emits, the size of a frame-streamed world (`#999`, `eabff61a`).**
`[Bounded Observation]`, 2026-11-23, a CPU count over every frame of the seven reference films' captured tick ranges at `320 x 200` with `185` viewport rows, no render and no timing: the probe plans the world as the upload does (one chunk on every film), walks it on each frame's camera, checks on every frame that its slices are `collect_world_meshes`'s, in order, and counts each emitted node's vertex window, indices and outline indices.
The largest frame emits `75,576` vertices, `76,008` indices and `149,956` outline indices (`INTRO` tick 3635): `2.88` MiB at `28` B a vertex and `4` B an index or outline index (`3.74` MiB with the `12` B apex), against `LOCATION`'s `333.75` MiB of baked world buffers ([`lavapipe-render-workers.md` § 3](lavapipe-render-workers.md#3-memory-per-worker)).
`LOCATION`'s largest frame is `60,838`, `62,712` and `116,832` (tick 5151) and its mean `7,013.8` vertices, `8,140.3` indices and `8,812.3` outline indices (about `0.26` MB); the ground slices alone peak at `14,935` vertices (`TRAINTNL` 101), and a per-frame prop-runs table at `987` pieces (`LOCATION` 3824) against the world's `31,441` runs.
These are the maxima of these films' captured ranges at this size and viewport, not a bound for any film or lens.
#1093 made the stream the only world path; its loop, memory and store figures are in [`lavapipe-render-time.md` § 1](lavapipe-render-time.md#1-changes-that-cut-render-time) and [`lavapipe-render-workers.md` § 3](lavapipe-render-workers.md#3-memory-per-worker).
_Source: [world stream sizes a frame](../../work/2026-11/999_frame-streamed-world/journal.md#2026-11-23-world-stream-sizes-a-frame-on-the-seven-reference-films), patch [`world-stream-probe.patch`](../../work/2026-11/999_frame-streamed-world/world-stream-probe.patch), [as this note stated it](../../work/2026-11/distill-lavapipe-render-time-3/journal.md#2026-11-23-world-stream-sizes-a-frame-as--3-stated-it-999)._

**No placement lies in the shared `WORLD_BASE` and `WORLD_DECAL` slices on the seven reference films (`#1090`, `25eb9586`).**
`[Bounded Observation]`, 2026-11-26, a CPU count of each film's whole world build as `build_world` makes it and `WorldPlacements::new` reads it, run with the parity render's own arguments, no renderer and no timing; the patch returns before playback, so the counts are not of a rendered tick and the captured range does not bound them.
On each of the seven films no placement record's `MeshSpans::base` or `::decal` run is non-empty, so none holds an index in the `WORLD_BASE` run (indices `0..342`) or the `WORLD_DECAL` run (`342..360`), the same on every film, and `WorldPlacements::split`, which takes its cuts out of those slices from such spans (`WorldPlacements::spans`, through `cut_out`), has nothing to cut at any tick; each placement with mesh spans has nodes of its own (`MeshSpans::nodes` non-empty).
Records with mesh spans, of all records: `CTRY` 306 of 316, `INTRO` 10,723 of 26,386, `LAND` 834 of 845, `BALLOON` 1,589 of 8,983, `TRAINTNL` 1,049 of 1,049, `BOMB` 970 of 1,158, `LOCATION` 35,481 of 56,585.
This repeats the check `#920` ran (Ruled out), reproducing its zero on `LOCATION` and `INTRO` and matching it on the other five; records of a placement the walk prunes as far from every camera eye are absent from the counts, as from the world mesh.
For `#999`'s byte-identical test (design note section 6, open call 8) none of the seven films exercises a stream builder's span-per-placement range: the test over them needs none, and such a range, if built, would be covered by a synthetic fixture, not a film; this note decides no design.
The world totals differ slightly from `#920`'s and `#999`'s (`INTRO` `128,772` nodes and `819,745` vertices against `128,768` and `819,265`; `LOCATION` `35,481` records with mesh spans against `35,479`); `[Hypothesis]` the OP_BLOCK cube change (`#1032`, `1e1d5680`) between their trees and this one added them, not isolated.
_Source: [placements cut out of the shared slices](../../work/2026-11/1090_placement-cut-probe/journal.md#2026-11-26-placements-cut-out-of-the-shared-slices-on-the-seven-reference-films), patch [`world-cuts-probe.patch`](../../work/2026-11/1090_placement-cut-probe/world-cuts-probe.patch), driven by [`probe_world_cuts.py`](../../work/2026-11/1090_placement-cut-probe/probe_world_cuts.py), [as this note stated it](../../work/2026-11/distill-lavapipe-render-time-3/journal.md#2026-11-26-placements-in-the-shared-world-slices-on-the-seven-reference-films-as--3-stated-it-1090)._

## Ruled out

* **Coalescing world base slices (#603, #622):** in node creation order no slice continues the previous one's index range (#603); laid out in walk order they merge (Span B 1,170 to 197 base draws a frame, the 28 stores byte-identical), but Span B's lavapipe loop fell only 4.4% to 4.8% over 16 pairs, under the 5% gate, the whole film's did not resolve, and peak memory rose transiently during the upload (a second copy), 11.4 MiB on lavapipe and 68.6 to 76.0 MiB on the RTX 4070; a revival needs an upload that reorders without a second copy; [#622 journal](../../work/2026-11/622_walk-order-indices/journal.md#2026-11-22-the-whole-film-span-a-on-the-rtx-4070-peak-memory-and-the-decision), [patch](../../work/2026-11/622_walk-order-indices/walk-order-layout.patch).
* **Un-baking the world's static props for a per-placement cull (#920):** skipping the world's base and decal draws, the saving's upper bound, cuts the whole `LOCATION` loop 49.0%, but no placement on any of the seven reference films has geometry in the shared `WORLD_BASE` or `WORLD_DECAL` run (342 and 18 terrain indices): each lies in nodes made for it alone, already culled on their own boxes, so a per-placement frustum test removes 0% of the 35,456,495 base and decal vertices drawn over the film, against a 30% bar; [journal](../../work/2026-11/distill-lavapipe-render-time/journal.md#2026-11-22-optimization-verdicts-as-of-f6976e67).
* **The world draws' Span B lavapipe cost as pixel work or as the base outlines (#920):** a 1-pixel scissor left 88.2% of Span B's loop and the base outlines alone saved 11.1%, though over the whole film the scissor recovers 26.1% and the base outlines 23.1%; [journal](../../work/2026-11/distill-lavapipe-render-time/journal.md#2026-11-22-where-the-world-base-and-decal-draws-cost-sits-920).
* **The per-fragment `prop_shape` lookup as the world draws' lavapipe cost (#920):** a constant saves 10.8% of the whole film's loop against the 49.0% the base and decal draws take; [journal](../../work/2026-11/distill-lavapipe-render-time/journal.md#2026-11-22-what-the-per-fragment-prop_shape-lookup-costs-920).
* **Batching the world's decal slices by screen box to cut their pipeline switches (`#1000`):** `[Bounded Observation]`, 2026-11-22, a CPU count on `7bfc6cca` plus [`probe-decal-batch.patch`](../../work/2026-11/1000_decal-batch-probe/probe-decal-batch.patch), no timing: with each node box grown 1 pixel, batching leaves `54.2%` of the whole `LOCATION` film's decal switches in place (section 1), most of them, which closes the issue by its own rule ("if batching leaves most switches in place, close this issue") at the gate's scope, the whole film (the owner's 2026-11-23 ruling on scope, no renderer change; Span B, which keeps `45.8%`, is context with nothing filed).
  If the saving is proportional to the switches removed (not measured), scaling `#920`'s `7.7%` grouping saving (2 rounds, not re-measured) puts it at about `3.5%` of the loop (`7.7%` times `45.8%`), or `3.7%` weighting each span's removed share by its own saving (`955 ms` on Span B, `1,122.5 ms` elsewhere; `996 ms` of `27,062.5 ms`), against the issue's `5%` gate (inferences, not timings); boxes not grown, outside the issue's conservative design, bound it at `4.3%` and `4.6%` and do not trigger its one-point rule.
  An order buffer that makes decal draw order commutative, not probed, could reach most of the `7.7%`, but needs a second order target beside the world's depth; [`#999`](https://github.com/neilsh/stunt-reel/issues/999)'s design note cites this close; [decal pipeline switches](../../work/2026-11/1000_decal-batch-probe/journal.md#2026-11-22-decal-pipeline-switches-greedy-batching-would-leave), [the owner's ruling on scope](../../work/2026-11/1000_decal-batch-probe/journal.md#2026-11-23-the-owners-ruling-on-scope), [as Ruled out stated it](../../work/2026-11/distill-lavapipe-render-time-2/journal.md#2026-11-23-batching-decal-slices-by-screen-box-as-ruled-out-stated-it-1000).
* **The queue submit's own overhead as a lever on the whole `LOCATION` film's lavapipe loop (`#1002`):** lavapipe's submit path takes `2.1%` of its queue thread's samples and `1.5%` of the main thread's, under the 5% bar, and `wgpu`'s `5.7%` on the main thread is encoding, done while the main thread would otherwise wait on the queue; [journal](../../work/2026-11/1002_lavapipe-world-profile/journal.md#2026-11-23-where-windows-lavapipe-spends-the-loop-on-the-whole-location-film).

## Open questions

1. What inside lavapipe makes the world decal slices' fill-to-outline alternation cost Span B's loop: grouping saves 955 ms of 2,377 ms, attributed to the switches by elimination, and the rest of the world's draws cost 779 ms more.
   `#1002`'s profile (section 2) makes storing the fragment state the largest single part of Span B's loop and the whole film's world cost mostly line setup, vertex work and rasterization, consistent with the whole film's lean toward outlines and pixels (2 rounds); since the stores a frame were not counted, whether a state store or binning flush at the switch is the cost, and what the 779 ms is, stay open.
   Which of the profile's levers above 5% of the whole `LOCATION` film's loop a renderer change can cut without moving a pixel is open too; each would justify an issue, none filed without the owner: the rasterizers' work (cutting per-fragment work in the plane shader, after open question 2); the draw module's vertex work and triangle setup (the latter near the bar; cutting the world vertices sent to the draw module); line setup and binning (drawing the world's outlines with fewer or cheaper line primitives at the same pixels); and the fragment-state store with the other state changes (cutting the stores a frame, which by Mesa 25.2.8's source follow fragment-visible binds and pipeline switches; batching the world decals by screen box is ruled out, `#1000`).
   _Source: [where Windows lavapipe spends the loop on the whole LOCATION film](../../work/2026-11/1002_lavapipe-world-profile/journal.md#2026-11-23-where-windows-lavapipe-spends-the-loop-on-the-whole-location-film), [open question 1 as it stood](../../work/2026-11/distill-lavapipe-render-time-2/journal.md#2026-11-23-open-question-1-as-it-stood-at-46e5b20c)._
   The memory rise during rendering is not these draws either: its timing is consistent with lavapipe filling the world's device buffers at the first submit ([`lavapipe-render-workers.md` § 3](lavapipe-render-workers.md#3-memory-per-worker), `[Supported Inference]` from other figures), though it is about half of the world's 333.75 MiB of buffers; untested.
   Since #1093 removed those buffers, the rise from the world mesh's release to the last frame of a `LOCATION` render of ticks 12-120 on Windows lavapipe fell from `101.7..102.7` to `24.2..27.0` MiB (3 runs each, `--report-memory`), consistent with that timing (`[Supported Inference]`).
   _Source: [#1093's gates](../../work/2026-11/999_frame-streamed-world/journal.md#2026-11-28-stage-c-the-stream-as-the-only-world-path-loop-memory-and-stores)._
2. Whether a per-vertex `shape_id` (or a per-instance one) costs less than the 10.8% of the whole `LOCATION` film's lavapipe loop the constant saved: what a carried id adds to the vertex fetch and the upload, and how much of the saving is the shapes plane compressing differently, are not measured.
