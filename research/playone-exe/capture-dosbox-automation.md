# `PLAYONE.EXE`: Capture with dosbox-automation (Frame-Locked Video)

The DOSBox-X debugger harness is in [`capture.md`](capture.md) and its speed and repeatability limits are in [`capture-speed.md`](capture-speed.md). This note evaluates a different capture tool: [dosbox-automation](https://github.com/dosbox-automation/dosbox-automation/) 0.85.1 (a DOSBox Staging fork with a REST and Lua API, GPL-2.0-or-later) recording lossless video of `CTRY.FLM`, with a per-frame film clock read from guest memory.

`[Bounded Observation]`, 2026-10-30, `CTRY.FLM` only, 16 logical cores, `cpu_cycles = 8000`, `core = normal`, three runs of DOSBox-X video and nine of dosbox-automation. No tracked code changed except the tool pin in [`mise.toml`](../../mise.toml); the driver scripts were scratch work under the data root and are not committed. Row in [`../parity/log.md`](../parity/log.md).

---

## 1. What the Tool Offers

* **Install:** `"github:dosbox-automation/dosbox-automation" = 0.85.1` in `mise.toml`, Windows zip, pinned by SHA-256; `mise exec -- dosbox` runs it.
* **Control:** REST on `127.0.0.1:<webserver_port>` with a bearer token (`DOSBOX_API_TOKEN`, which must be 64 hex characters or the emulator discards it and generates its own), and a Lua script on the emulation thread (`POST /api/v1/script/load`, `/start`, `GET /status`; results in `dosbox.output`).
* **Guest access:** `GET /api/v1/memory/<seg>/<off>/<len>` (raw, up to 128 MiB), `GET /api/v1/cpu/state` (all registers, `DS` included), `GET /api/v1/io/port`; in Lua `dosbox.mem_read_word(seg, off)`, `dosbox.frame()` and `dosbox.wait_frames(n)`.
* **Capture:** `dosbox.capture_start()` / `capture_stop()` (or `POST /api/v1/capture/video/start` with `mode: raw`) records lossless AVI (ZMBV) at the emulated display rate, one video frame per emulated frame.
* **Absent:** the debugger. Per the OpenAPI document `debugger` is `false`: no breakpoints, no pause or step, no hooks on an address or a port.
* **Mount policy:** a mount under a system path (the user's `Temp`) is refused; the staged mount and conf live under the data root (`<data root>/spike/`).

## 2. Frame-Locked Capture

`[Bounded Observation]`. One Lua script drives a whole run: wait for the absolute emulated frame 1200 (`dosbox.frame()` counts from emulator start), `capture_start()`, press `c`, Down and Enter at fixed frame offsets (20 frames before each press, 5 held), then once per frame append `dosbox.frame()`, `film_tick` (`DS:0x4896`) and `global_tick` (`DS:0x22B8`) to `dosbox.output` in chunks of 100, and `capture_stop()` after 3,100 logged frames.

* **`DS` is `0x0F46` here**, not DOSBox-X's `0x1575` of [`capture.md`](capture.md) § 3: the image loads at a different paragraph. The offsets are the same. `/api/v1/cpu/state` reports `DS` at the moment of the call, so read it while the film plays: after a long hold it reported `0x17C9`, and an `INTRO.FLM` run that took that value as its constant logged garbage (`film_tick` up to 65,535). `0x0F46` is right for both `CTRY.FLM` and `INTRO.FLM`.
* **Nine runs are bit-identical.** Five sequential and four started together (ports 8387 to 8390): 3,175 video frames each (the 75 frames before logging starts plus 3,100 logged), zero frames that differ by hash, zero differing rows in the tick logs, and the clock ends at 1,342 in all. An earlier set that started the capture and the keys by wall-clock delay (REST, three runs) had two identical runs and one that began a frame later and then sampled the film at different instants: only 11 of 206 shared timer values had identical frames.
* **No drops under this parallel load.** The logs hold 3,100 consecutive frame numbers (no gaps), `film_tick` never decreases, and the four parallel runs equal the sequential ones. Four instances on 16 logical cores is light; a heavier load was not tried.
* **Video frame *j* is emulated frame 1200 + *j*.** Against the decoded HUD timer, offset 0 minimises the gap between `2 * film_tick` and the timer (in centiseconds). At the first frame of each new timer value the timer equals `film_tick / 50` in 348 of 424 cases (0 cs), 58 at -2 cs and 18 at +2 cs. Between render loops the live tick runs 0 to 3 ticks ahead of the displayed timer, which is the render loop (about 17 loops per second) against the 50 Hz clock.
* **Frames per timer value.** 3 to 6 video frames per HUD timer value at 70.087 Hz, with a median of 4; no run shows a stall longer than 7 frames.

### 4.1 Second Round: Seven Questions Answered

`[Bounded Observation]`, 2026-10-30. Five Haiku subagents and one Sonnet subagent ran fully specified briefs (scripts and logs under `out\inv1` to `inv6` in the data root); the main session re-ran the page-versus-video comparison itself (`w1`, `w2`, `w3`) and read the DS logs from disk. The other subagents' tables are as they reported them.

* **7,000 cycles matches DOSBox-X's loop count, not its pixels.** Normal core, `CTRY.FLM`: 6,500 / 7,000 / 7,500 cycles give 344 / 370 / 396 render loops against DOSBox-X's 369 (three DOSBox-X runs, all 369). Two runs at 7,000 are bit-identical (3,175 frames, equal tick logs). Aligned by HUD timer, none of 58 shared timer values has a byte-identical pair. 96.3% of pixels are within 1 per channel and 14 of 58 best pairs differ by at most 1, which points at the 6-bit to 8-bit conversion (the DOSBox-X rounding was not read); about 3.7% of pixels differ widely and were not located. Only 58 of about 370 timer values are shared, so a comparison needs a tolerance and a nearest-frame search.
* **The dynamic core is not interchangeable.** At 8,000 cycles it gives 417 loops against the normal core's 424, with two bit-identical runs per core. The film clocks agree from frame 1276 to 2066, then differ by 1 to 2 ticks from frame 2067. Pictures already differ at frame 1280. Of 370 differing timer-paired frames, 341 are different content and 29 equal a neighbour. Use the normal core. Whether the dynamic core's pictures are wrong or only drawn at other instants was not resolved.
* **Memory and parallel limits.** One `INTRO.FLM` run to 200 frames past the hold (5,832 video frames, 83 s emulated) took 106 s wall (1.28x), 18.9 MB of AVI (227 KB per emulated second) and a 207 MB peak working set. Sixteen and 24 parallel `CTRY.FLM` runs on 16 logical cores and 31 GB of RAM were all bit-identical to the reference with zero dropped frames; wall time per run stayed about 69 to 73 s, and each instance holds about 0.21 GB (40 would need about 8.4 GB).
* **A page and palette read fits one frame.** In Lua, `dosbox.mem_read(0xA000, 0, 64000)` and `dosbox.mem_read(0x0F46, 0x2768, 768)` (the working palette, 6-bit units; base palette at `DS:0x2468`) in the same step see one instant, because Lua blocks the emulation. The page converts to hex with `raw:gsub(".", H)`, where `H` maps each byte to its `%02x` string; that loop is in C and fits the instruction limit. The game draws straight into `A000`. Runs are bit-identical (26 of 26 spread samples, then 100 of 100 consecutive frames). No render-complete flag turned up in DGROUP. The output store holds about 15 MB, which is about 100 samples per run; a long film needs one run per window of frames. The conf and the game mount must sit together under the data root, or the mount is refused (§ 6).
* **Frame 2400 is a HUD offset, and the offsets are not systematic.** Two runs of frames 2390 to 2409 are identical (page, palette and meta, 20 of 20). The page equals the video frame at `F - 1200` exactly in 14 of 20 samples and one frame earlier (`F - 1201`) in 4 more; at frame 2400 the 79 differing pixels lie in rows 181 to 184, the timer digits. Two samples (2393: 757 pixels in rows 103 to 184, 2405: 10 pixels in rows 104 to 108) match no video frame, which is the render loop writing `A000` while it is scanned out. In a fade window (frames 2690 to 2709) 16 of 20 samples have an exact video frame within two frames and four (2696, 2697, 2701, 2702) have none, best differing in 3,500 to 4,200 pixels; PLAYONE uploads the DAC in two retrace-gated halves (CS:0x7B70), so a video frame can mix two palettes. The mismatch is therefore the page being read at a frame boundary during a render loop (about 17 per second against 70 frames per second), not a palette change at frame 2400.
* **DS depends on the code segment.** In every REST sample of both films `film_tick` never goes backwards and equals `global_tick` before the hold, with `film_tick` ending at 1,342 and 3,822. DS is 0x0F46 while `CS` is 0x01F5, and 0x17C9 while `CS` is 0x0A19; 0x336B and 0x430B also occur with `CS` 0x01F5. So 0x17C9 is the data segment of another code module, and the Lua driver's explicit `0x0F46:offset` reads are correct throughout. What lives at segment 0x17C9 was not identified (its first bytes are not a PSP or an MCB).

### 4.2 Third Round: INTRO, DAC Rounding and a Render Counter

`[Bounded Observation]` for the measurements, `[Supported Inference]` for the DAC reading, 2026-10-30, same machine, main session only (scratch scripts under the data root).

* **The page and working-palette read holds for `INTRO.FLM`.** 7,000 cycles, normal core, 97 samples at frames 1300 to 6580 step 55 (the § 6 snippet with `KBD_i`): 83 pages render exactly to a video frame within one frame (79 at offset 0) through `DS:0x2768` and `round(x*255/63)`. The 14 that do not were not all inspected; two (frames 2345 and 4160) differ in about 59,000 of 64,000 pixels at every offset, which fits a cut or palette change between the page read and the video frame, and the rest differ in the HUD rows or in a band of rows, as a fade or a mid-render read would. The run took 99 s wall for 5,382 video frames and 97 pages, and the lossless AVI was 17.4 MB. A full-film dump needs one run per 97 samples (the output store limit).
* **DOSBox-X expands the 6-bit DAC differently, by exactly 1 on 10 of 64 levels.** Its `dacexpand` in `vga_dac.cpp` computes `(v << 2) | (v >> 4)`; dosbox-automation's video equals `round(v * 255 / 63)`. They agree except at levels 11 to 15 and 48 to 52, where DOSBox-X is one lower or one higher by `|d| = 1`. So the 1-per-channel shift in § 4.1 is the DAC expansion, from the source and the exact-match result above; it was not measured on a DOSBox-X page. The 3.7% of pixels that differ by more than 1 per channel are not explained by it.
* **No per-render counter was found in `DGROUP`.** Diffing the 64 KB `DS:0000..FFFE` once per frame over 90 frames of `CTRY.FLM` (frames 1500 to 1589) shows the page changing in 61 of 90 frames, because the guest draws straight into `A000` over several frames rather than flipping a finished page. The only byte that stepped by +1 at about the loop rate was `DS:0x578C`, which a 2,200-frame log rejected: at 8,000 cycles it changed 268 times before the hold against 424 render loops, and it drops by 9 twenty-six times, so it is a cycling index, not a loop count. A render-complete signal therefore has to come from the code, not from a data diff.

### 4.3 Fourth Round: The Five Open Questions

2026-10-30, four Sonnet subagents with self-contained briefs (scratch scripts under the session scratchpad, nothing in the repo). Their numbers were not rerun in the main session, except the `set_palette` disassembly, which was checked against the unpacked image.

* **Static: three `0x03DA` polls, one in Mode 13h.** `[Supported Inference]`, static only. The image offsets are `0x7B70` `set_palette` (the vtable entry `DS:0x2252`), a dead wait at `0x7B5E`, and a planar page flip at `0x649B`. `set_palette` runs `out 3C8h,0` then `call 7B79`; the callee waits for retrace end and start and then does `rep outsb` of 384 bytes. Its `ret` at `0x7B8E` returns into `0x7B79`, so the routine runs a second retrace-gated 384-byte half, then returns to its caller. That is the "two halves" of the DAC upload. `0x7B8E` is `0F17:0C5E` in the notebook coordinates (`0x7B8E - 0x6F30`). Whether the `BP 0D51:0C5E` of [`capture.md`](capture.md) § 3 named the same bytes is not established: `0D51:0C5E` is linear `0xE16E`, which maps to image `0x592E` or `0xC21E` under the recorded load bases. The `render_frame_flush` call in [`memory.md`](memory.md) § 3.1 stays unverified.
* **No retrace wait before the `A000` write.** Vtable entry `0x738C` swaps the two `0xFA00`-byte buffers `[22CA]`/`[22CC]` and diff-copies the new frame to `A000` (`repe cmpsw`/`rep movsw`, `0x73A8` to `0x73D6`, then sprites to `0x7415`). The routine ends at `0x7417`, the render-complete candidate. It never waits for retrace, so tearing is expected, as the tear class below finds. The `0x7417` end point was read from the listing and not tried as a breakpoint.
* **Segments `0x0A19` and `0x17C9` are the same image.** `[Supported Inference]`. The image loads at paragraph `0x1F5` (`0x0F46 - 0xD51`). `CS = 0x0A19` is `0x1F5 + 0x824` (image offset `0x8240`, the VM code segment) and `DS = 0x17C9` is `0x1F5 + 0x15D4` (image offset `0x15D40`), a second data and work segment of about 111 KB that holds the anchor table at `0x15D44` and the VM variables at `0x8120` to `0x8128`. `mov ax,15D4h` / `mov ds,ax` is at image `0xB012` in the VM dispatch. It is not an overlay, EMS or a second module. The relocation table's other segment words (`0x1DE7`, `0x1DEA`, `0x20F6`) were not decoded; `0x336B` and `0x430B` fall beyond the image end, so they are DOS-allocated `[Hypothesis]`.
* **Fade: low half new, high half previous.** `[Bounded Observation]`. During the INTRO fade (frames 6158 to 6390, about 19 steps, one per 12 frames) each step makes one video frame whose entries 0 to 127 come from the new palette and entries 128 to 255 from the previous one, as two retrace-gated halves predict. It fits 180 of 180 sampled frames (windows 6150 to 6239 and 6300 to 6389) with no mismatching pixel; a whole-palette model fails the 32 mixed samples by 192 to 2,539 pixels. A partly written working palette (163 entries at frame 6336) was not what the DAC high half held. Frames 2345, 3407 and 4160 are single whole-palette cuts, not fades. Palette ids came from the `DS:0x2768` scan, not a DAC read, and ties on unused indices are arbitrary.
* **The 14 unmatched INTRO samples are explained.** `[Bounded Observation]`. Four are palette races: at 2345 and 4160 the page is 92% fill index 193, which the working palette at that frame paints green but the video shows black, and the page with the working palette from frame *f-1* matches video frames *f-1* and *f* exactly; at 6195 and 6360 (fade steps) the page indices are unchanged from *f-1* to *f+1* while the working palette moves. Ten are mid-render reads: four large tears (the top 59 to 84 rows match video frames *f* to *f+5*, the rest match *f-1* or earlier), three bottom bands (12 to 49 rows) and three tiny tears (1 to 12 rows). For a scorer: drop or retry at *f+2* a sample whose working palette differs from the previous frame's; accept a torn page if each row matches some video frame in *f-2* to *f+4*, or retry at *f+1*. The two agents disagree on the palette lead: the fade run saw page-to-video offset 0 on matched samples, the unmatched run says the working palette leads the DAC by about 2 frames and did not test the working palette at *f-2*.
* **The 3.7% is mostly a different instant of the same film.** `[Bounded Observation]`. Against the existing DOSBox-X capture `captures/vault-ctry/frames/001` (124 index planes), 41 `film_tick` values were read in dosbox-automation (8,000 cycles). Before the fade (30 ticks, 10 to 989) the palettes are identical and every wide pixel is an index difference, 6.9% of the page on average (892 to 16,487 pixels). In the fade and tail (11 ticks, 1,000 to 1,331) nine ticks have 150 to 230 differing DAC entries, and 96% of the wide pixels have the same index but another palette, a different fade phase. There is no border, overscan or HUD pattern (rows 176 to 199 hold under 4%). Bursts of consecutive pages show the page lags the clock word by a different number of ticks in each emulator: DOSBox-X tick 110 equals dosbox-automation ticks 104 to 105 exactly, tick 770 equals 767 exactly, tick 384 differs by 14 pixels at 380. Ticks 220, 495 and 660 leave 653, 489 and 1,082 pixels with no exact page in the window; that is Unknown. DOSBox-X run-to-run variance was not measured, and video export scaling was not tested because both sides are 320x200 index pages.

### 4.4 The Production Harness, Live

2026-10-30, the committed `mise run capture-ref`, `make-all` and the plan code ([ADR 0025](../../docs/adr/0025-capture-runs-are-plans-not-sessions.md), [`plan.py`](../../python/stunt_reel/tac/plan.py)), run from the main session. Twelve stale `dosbox-x.exe` processes were running throughout, so seconds are indicative.

* **`CTRY.FLM` captures end to end.** `[Bounded Observation]`. The trace pass stopped by itself at the end hold (1,915 frames); 113 frames at an 11-tick stride came back over three dump passes, each replay's trace equal to the first, and the run fits in under 10 minutes. Against the DOSBox-X reference `captures/vault-ctry/frames/001`, 0.951 of the first 60,000 page indices agree over the 37 film ticks the two share within one tick ([`../parity/method.md`](../parity/method.md) § 3.1). Nothing was scored with `stuntreel parity`.
* **Ninety pages per pass do not fit.** `[Bounded Observation]`. A dump pass of 90 pages (about 11.5 MB of hex in `dosbox.output`) failed with the Lua error `not enough memory`; 40 per pass works (`MAX_DUMPS`). So the limit is the Lua heap, not the 15 MB output store.
* **Replays are not always identical.** `[Bounded Observation]`. `COUNT.FLM` (`siarchive/Films/FLMOnly`, archive from `make-all`) traced five times with one script on the `capture` profile gave four different traces: the film clock at a given emulated frame differs by one tick in 10 to 18 of 157 frames, and the page-changed flag moves with it; tick 20 falls on frame 1300 in every run. `[mixer] nosound = true` still gave three variants in four runs, so the host audio device is not the cause. `sbtype = none` made five runs identical, but `PLAYONE` then never started the film (clock word 14,650, global tick 0), so that is no control. `CTRY.FLM` replays were identical in every run so far (§ 2, and four passes above). The source of the jitter is Unknown; Patch #3 ships a different `PLAYONE.EXE` (97,790 bytes against the corpus's 102,703, which the demo and the full game share), and the [DOSBox page of SI Central](https://armknechted.com/sicentral/newpage/dosbox.html) says some films only play in one of the two, so `COUNT.FLM` may need the patched build. The plan now labels each page with the tick its own pass read and refuses a frame more than 2 ticks from the first pass, so jitter moves a sample but never mislabels it.
* **`INTRO.FLM` captures end to end, slowly one pass at a time.** `[Bounded Observation]`. 315 frames over one trace and eight dump passes of up to 40, every replay's trace equal to the first, in 17 min 0 s run one after another (each pass replays the boot and the whole film, about 110 s). That is past the batch survey's 900 s capture timeout, so dump passes now run side by side, up to four at once (`DEFAULT_PARALLEL_PASSES`, since raised to eight; § 4.4), and the frames are split into even passes. Against the DOSBox-X reference `vault-intro/frames/001`, 0.872 of page indices agree over 38 ticks shared within one tick, falling to 0.69 to 0.77 after tick 3,170; the late film moves the camera fast, where a page that lags its clock by a different amount in each emulator (§ 4.3) costs more.
* **One conf file per launch.** `[Established]`. The first parallel run failed with `GuestTimeout`: all passes shared one stage and wrote one `dosbox.conf`, so a later launch overwrote the port an earlier one was about to read and that emulator served the wrong port. Each launch now writes `dosbox-<port>.conf`. With that fix `CTRY.FLM` captured 121 frames in 112 s wall (four passes side by side) against about 9 minutes before, with no tick moved in any pass.
* **45,000 cycles is where the render rate saturates.** `[Bounded Observation]`, a Sonnet subagent's sweep of trace passes on the normal core, raw traces checked in the main session. `PLAYONE` draws a new page on 46% of emulated frames at 7,000 cycles, 79% at 15,000 (the figure the [SI Central DOSBox page](https://armknechted.com/sicentral/newpage/dosbox.html) suggests for play), 98% at 30,000 and 99% from 35,000 to about 100,000 (`CTRY.FLM` ticks under 1,000); the cap is one page per emulated frame, about 1.4 per film tick. Above 100,000 the rate falls (88% at 150,000) and `cpu_cycles = max` gives 95% on `CTRY.FLM` and 80% on `INTRO.FLM`, against 99% at 45,000. Three runs each at 20,000, 30,000 and 45,000 gave byte-identical traces. The dynamic core saturates at the same place and agrees with the normal core within about 1% from 15,000 up (45,000: 98.5% against 98.7%; 35,000: 99.2% against 98.7%), and three runs each at 35,000 and 60,000 were byte-identical too, but its traces are not the normal core's; dynamic at `max` is broken for sampling (47% of frames, a 2,017-frame trace instead of about 1,908), which does not touch `make-all`, whose archives at that setting are byte-identical (`../makeone-exe/driving.md` § 9). Wall time is the same at every value (about 55 s for `CTRY.FLM`, 114 s for `INTRO.FLM`), so emulated time paces the run. The `capture` profile and `play` use 45,000 cycles (the core is in the next item); 7,000 was chosen only to match DOSBox-X's render-loop count. A `CTRY.FLM` capture at 45,000 agrees with DOSBox-X reference `001` on 0.958 of indices over 10 shared ticks and with the 7,000-cycle capture `002` on 0.952 over 27.
* **The dynamic core costs 40% less host CPU at the same rate.** `[Bounded Observation]`. Host CPU seconds of one `dosbox.exe` over a `CTRY.FLM` trace pass (process times from `GetProcessTimes`, 54.4 to 54.7 s wall in every run): normal core at 45,000 cycles 21.1 and 21.3 s (0.39 of a core); dynamic at 45,000 13.4, 12.2 and 12.7 s (0.22 to 0.24), its three traces identical; dynamic at 35,000 18.8 then 12.0 s (the first run an outlier); normal at 7,000 17.6 s. The rate is the same (above), so the `capture` profile, which `play` shares, is now the dynamic core at 45,000 cycles and dump passes run up to eight at once (about two cores). With that profile `CTRY.FLM` captured 121 frames in 113 s (`captures/vault-ctry/frames/003`), and `INTRO.FLM` captured 347 frames in nine passes in 453 s on the normal core with four at once (`vault-intro/frames/003`), inside the batch's 900 s.

### 4.5 Torn Pages and the DAC Lag

2026-10-31, the `capture` profile (dynamic core, 45,000 cycles), unpatched `PLAYONE.EXE`, scratch Lua over every emulated frame of `CTRY.FLM` and `INTRO.FLM`. Each frame's rows 0–191 of `A000` were compared, in 24 bands of 8 rows, with the two composed pages, and a frame with a band only one page matches and a band only the other matches counts as torn; its working palette was compared with the previous frame's.

* **A page read in the frame callback can be torn.** `[Supported Inference]` for the mechanism. The callback runs at an emulated-frame boundary set by emulated time, and the `A000` update (`0x738C`, `repe cmpsw`/`rep movsw` then sprites, no retrace wait) takes tens of thousands of guest cycles, so a boundary can land inside it. The game keeps two composed pages, their segments in `DS:0x22CA` and `0x22CC` (`0x336B` and `0x430B` here), swapped every render; after a finished copy rows 0–191 of `A000` equal one of them. Torn pages show a clean split: top rows from one buffer, the rest from the other.
* **How often.** `[Bounded Observation]`, 24 bands of 8 rows per page:

  | | frames | changed film frames torn | all frames torn | picked frames of reference `004` torn |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | `CTRY.FLM` | 1,908 | 4.1% (66; 71 at row level) | 3.5% | 3 of 121 (4 at row level) |
  | `INTRO.FLM` | 5,384 | 2.9% (127) | 2.4% | 7 of 346 |

  The torn picks of `004`, by emulated frame: `CTRY.FLM` 1677, 1816 and 1986, plus 1662 at row level only (2 of 192 rows); `INTRO.FLM` 3382, 4604, 5053, 5068, 5269, 5284 and 5794. In every `CTRY.FLM` case but one, frame *f* + 1 is clean. The rate should scale as 1/cycles (REST snapshots at 7,000 cycles gave 10 of 97); not swept.
* **The DAC lags the working palette.** `[Bounded Observation]`, one `CTRY.FLM` fade, five palette steps, frames 2680–2729 against a lossless AVI decoded per palette half. At the best page-to-video alignment every step fits exactly one model: at the step's first frame *c* the DAC is the old palette, at *c* + 1 the low half is new and the high half old, from *c* + 2 both are new. So the DAC with page *f* is `low = W(f-1)`, `high = W(f-2)`, which settles the palette-lead question of § 4.3: one frame for the low half, two for the high.
* **Exposure in the `004` references.** `[Bounded Observation]`. No picked frame of `004` falls at *c* or *c* + 1 (0 of 121 and 0 of 346), because the page rarely changes there and `pick` takes changed pages only; 17 of 1,618 and 2 of 4,367 candidate frames are in the window.
* **What the harness does with it.** The trace carries a torn flag and a palette-changed flag per frame, `pick` skips torn frames and the two frames of each palette step, and a page its own dump pass reads as unusable is dropped ([`luagen.py`](../../python/stunt_reel/tac/luagen.py), [`plan.py`](../../python/stunt_reel/tac/plan.py)). Rebuilding the DAC from the two previous palettes was not built; it waits for a film whose fade leaves too few frames. A fourth flag marks a page whose rows 0–191 equal one composed page, and `meta.json` counts them as `composed_matches`: wrong `composed_pages` offsets for a build would otherwise read as a film with no tears, and a zero count, which `capture-ref` also prints a warning for, says the offsets read no real page.
* **Recaptured with the skip, the references did not improve.** `[Bounded Observation]`, 2026-10-31, `mise run capture-ref` on both films with the skip in place. The traces flagged exactly the investigation's counts: 66 of 1,908 `CTRY.FLM` frames torn and 64 palette steps, 127 of 5,384 `INTRO.FLM` frames torn and 65 steps; no dump pass dropped a page. Against one render, `vault-ctry/frames/005` scores SSIM 0.7800 (114 of 121) where `004` scores 0.7808, inside the 0.002 floor, and `vault-intro/frames/005` 0.5444 (277 of 345) where `004` scores 0.5488, past it. So the references stay on `004` ([`../parity/log.md`](../parity/log.md)). Why `INTRO.FLM` falls when torn pages are replaced by their clean neighbours is Unknown: the moved picks score against other render ticks, and the render's own error at those ticks may outweigh the tears.

## 5. Consequences

* **Determinism.** A frame-locked dosbox-automation capture is byte-repeatable where the DOSBox-X debugger runs vary by a few ticks (the baseline noise in [`capture-speed.md`](capture-speed.md) § 1), and it records every emulated frame, so no sampling stride decides which pages exist.
* **Exact tick per frame.** The log supplies `film_tick` for every video frame from guest memory, which the DOSBox-X video lacks ("carries no film tick", [`capture-speed.md`](capture-speed.md)). The tags are the tick at the end of each emulated frame, with no debugger latency (compare the 5.75-tick slice jitter in [`capture-speed.md`](capture-speed.md) § 2).
* **What video does not give.** The frames are RGB: index agreement, IoU on the index plane and fade-compensated scoring ([`../parity/method.md`](../parity/method.md)) need the palette indices. The video's colours are exactly the scaled DAC (§ 4), but two indices can share an RGB (one pair in four sampled frames), so the index plane is not always recoverable from video alone. The Lua page and working-palette read of § 4.1 supplies it at chosen frames, with the caveat that a page read in the middle of a render loop can match no video frame, and in a fade the real DAC lags the working palette by up to a frame in two halves. Index and IoU scoring uses the page and the working palette, not the video.
* **Built.** `stunt_reel/tac` now drives dosbox-automation only ([ADR 0025](../../docs/adr/0025-capture-runs-are-plans-not-sessions.md)): [`luagen.py`](../../python/stunt_reel/tac/luagen.py) writes the scripts, [`plan.py`](../../python/stunt_reel/tac/plan.py) runs the trace and dump passes, and [`framebuffer.py`](../../python/stunt_reel/tac/framebuffer.py) writes the capture. Byte-repeatability holds for `CTRY.FLM` but not for every film (§ 4.4), so a capture is labelled per page, not per plan.

## 6. Reference: the Lua Driver and the Timer Glyphs

The scratch scripts are not committed; this is what a driver needs. Launch `dosbox.exe -conf <conf>` with `DOSBOX_API_TOKEN` set to 64 hex characters, `[webserver] webserver_enabled = true`, `webserver_port = <port>`, a mount under the data root (not `Temp`) with the conf beside it (a conf in a distant folder gets `MOUNT: Blocked ... outside allowed directories`, the emulator sits at a DOS prompt and a stale `video0001.avi` can pass for a film; `mount_allowed_bases` is not a key) and `[capture] capture_dir`. Then `POST /api/v1/script/load` (body is the Lua, `text/plain`), `POST /api/v1/script/start`, and poll `GET /api/v1/script/status` until `state` is `completed`; the log is in `output`. The per-frame log must be written in chunks of 100 into separate `dosbox.output` keys; a 64,000-byte page converted to hex in one frame exceeds the Lua instruction limit.

```lua
local DS = 0x0F46                     -- DGROUP segment for this emulator (§ 2)
local out = dosbox.output
local function wait_until(f) while dosbox.frame() < f do dosbox.wait_frames(1) end end
wait_until(1200)                      -- an absolute emulated frame, so the start is boot-relative
dosbox.capture_start()                -- raw video; video frame j is emulated frame 1200 + j
out.cap_start_frame = dosbox.frame()
for _, k in ipairs({"KBD_c", "KBD_down", "KBD_enter"}) do   -- first letter of the film, Down, Enter
  dosbox.wait_frames(20); dosbox.key(k, true); dosbox.wait_frames(5); dosbox.key(k, false)
end
local chunk, n = {}, 0
for i = 1, 3100 do
  dosbox.wait_frames(1)
  chunk[#chunk + 1] = dosbox.frame() .. "," .. dosbox.mem_read_word(DS, 0x4896) .. "," .. dosbox.mem_read_word(DS, 0x22B8)
  if #chunk == 100 then out["k" .. string.format("%04d", n)] = table.concat(chunk, ";"); chunk = {}; n = n + 1 end
end
if #chunk > 0 then out["k" .. string.format("%04d", n)] = table.concat(chunk, ";") end
dosbox.capture_stop()
out.done = true
```

**Page and working palette at a chosen frame** (§ 4.1), replacing the per-frame loop body above:

```lua
local H = {}
for i = 0, 255 do H[string.char(i)] = string.format("%02x", i) end
for _, f in ipairs(frames) do                      -- at most 40 per run (MAX_DUMPS; 90 ran out of Lua memory)
  wait_until(f)
  local pg = dosbox.mem_read(0xA000, 0, 64000)     -- indices; the game draws straight into A000
  local wp = dosbox.mem_read(DS, 0x2768, 768)      -- working palette, 6-bit units
  out["s" .. f] = dosbox.frame() .. "," .. dosbox.mem_read_word(DS, 0x4896) .. "|" .. wp:gsub(".", H) .. "|" .. pg:gsub(".", H)
end
```

Rebuild RGB as `round(x * 255 / 63)` per palette byte. Build the Python side with `str.replace`, not `%`, since `%02x` collides with `%` formatting.

**HUD timer decoding.** The AVI is read with `ffmpeg -vf scale=320:200:flags=neighbor -pix_fmt rgb24` (DOSBox-X's is 640x400; dosbox-automation's is already 320x200). The timer is `MM:SS.cc` at rows 179 to 183, six 3x5 digit cells at columns 1, 5, 11, 15, 21 and 25 (the colon and dot sit between), with a pixel counted as ink when its RGB sum is below 60. **Ruled out**

* **A wall-clock start for dosbox-automation.** Capture start and keys by REST delays: one run in three started a frame late and sampled different instants; use frame-locked starts (§ 2).
* **Frame-for-frame match with DOSBox-X video.** Different guest speed at equal cycles (§ 3); at 7,000 cycles the loop counts agree (370 against 369) but no timer-aligned pair is byte-identical (§ 4.1).
* **The dynamic core as a drop-in for the normal core.** Its film clock departs by 1 to 2 ticks from frame 2067 and 341 of 370 differing frames are different content (§ 4.1).
* **A palette change at frame 2400.** The mismatch is a one-frame offset in the HUD timer rows (§ 4.1).
* **REST port reads for the DAC in a scoring loop.** About 5.75 s per palette and not frame-locked; the DGROUP working palette in the same Lua step replaces it (§ 4.1).
* **`Ctrl+Alt+F5` and `F11+I` as DOSBox-X video start through `SendInput`.** No file appeared in the capture directory; the menu command did (§ 3).
* **A cheaper tear test as the fix for `vault-intro`, `vault-location` and `vault-tod`.** With the band loop removed, `TOD.FLM` and `INTRO.FLM` (retail archive) still exhaust the budget near the 12,000-frame cap, because the film clock never holds above zero (§ 4.7).
* **A harness change since the `vault-intro` captures.** The earlier captures used the demo's `INTRO.ALL` and the unpatched build; the survey picks the retail archive and `NewPLAY`'s patched build (§ 4.7).
* **The engine files or the archive alone as the cause of the `TOD.FLM` and `INTRO.FLM` failures.** `NewPLAY`'s engine files play both films with the unpatched executable, and the retail archives play on the unpatched build; only the patched executable with the retail archive fails (§ 4.7, second round).
* **`LOCATION.FLM`'s archive or build as its cause.** It plays under the batch's own configuration; it is too long for one trace script (§ 4.7, second round).
