## 1. Parity Loop (Capture vs. Render)

The parity loop compares what the original player scans out against what the native engine rasterizes for the same film. All evidence lands under `build/parity/` (gitignored); `mise run capture-ref` drives the capture.

The capture side (control socket direction, frame gating on the post-retrace trap, dialog automation, load safety, the withdrawn `VRT` route) is documented once in [`capture.md`](playone-exe/capture.md); it is not repeated here. What remains is how the captured pages are read and compared:

* **Page extraction (`[Established]`):** `PLAYONE.EXE` scans out the Mode 13h page at `A000:0000` during playback (`VGA MODE` reports `disp-start=0h`). `MEMDUMPBIN A000:0000 FA00 <path>` writes the 64000-byte page; the debugger parses both addresses and lengths in hex, and resolves host paths only when given forward-slash absolute paths without quotes. DOS 8.3 normalization uppercases the written filename.
* **Palette (`[Established]`):** `VGA DACPAL` prints 8 entries per line as 6-hex-digit RGB triples in 6-bit DAC units, scaled to `0..=255` before luminance comparison with the capture scaling defined in [`palette-and-shading.md` § 1.1](formats/concepts/palette-and-shading.md#11-vga-hardware-dac-principles), which also records how it differs from the renderer's authored palette.
* **Comparator (`[Established]`):** `stuntreel parity` reduces both sides to Rec. 601 luminance, stretches $320 \times 200 \rightarrow 320 \times 240$ per `[I6]`, scores `compute_frame_ssim` (window 11, stride 8), and reports the IoU of each side's non-sky mask plus `contact_NNNN.png` sheets (`reference | rendered | |difference|`).
* **Tick counter (`[Established]`):** `parity` matches each capture to the render at the same `film_tick` (`DS:0x4896`, with `global_tick` from `DS:0x22B8`). Offsets and the live-sampling evidence (including the film-clock/global-counter offset, which is not constant): [`MEMORY_MAP.md` `# [MEM-FLM-TICK]`](../docs/MEMORY_MAP.md#-mem-flm-tick) and [`memory.md` § 3.2](playone-exe/memory.md#32-tick-counter-evidence).
* **Interval frame extraction (`[Established]`):** To prevent disk exhaustion and accelerate continuous regression auditing across long film choreographies, `stuntreel --out-dir <dir> --frame-step <N>` samples the 50 Hz timeline every $N$ ticks. `--frames <A..B>` is a half-open range of 1-based tick ordinals; extraction writes `tick_%05d.png` named by the 0-based 50 Hz tick rendered, which is the film clock `stuntreel parity` looks up. With `--sample-count` (or `--video`) the samples need not fall on whole ticks, so they are written as `sample_%04d.png` (1-based sample index) and are not parity inputs. When `--video` is also present, video sampling at `--fps` drives the loop and `--frame-step` does not apply.
* **Video container export (`[Established]`):** Complementing static PNG inspection, `stuntreel --video <path>` encodes rendered frame streams directly into standard container formats (`.mp4`, `.webm`, `.mkv`, `.avi`) via an asynchronous frame pipe to `ffmpeg`. Frames are sampled from the authored 50 Hz timeline at `--fps` (default $30$) with $4:3$ aspect correction ($320 \times 240$ per `[I6]`), interpolating between slice keyframes. `--frames <A..B>` selects a half-open range of 1-based tick ordinals. The container is trimmed to the authored span via ffmpeg's `-t`, so `CTRY.FLM` (140 slices, 1,342 ticks, $26.84\text{ s}$) samples 806 frames at $30\text{ fps}$; ffmpeg encodes 805 and reports $26.833\text{ s}$ (nearest whole frame; $26.84\text{ s}$ exactly at `--fps 50`). If `ffmpeg` is not detected on `PATH`, the engine terminates gracefully with an informative diagnostic without abnormal termination. Implemented under `T11` in [`TEAM_SHEET.md`](../docs/TEAM_SHEET.md).

---
* **Fade floor (`[Bounded Observation]`):** `PLAYONE` fades its palette to black over the end of `CTRY.FLM` (about tick 990 to 1337), and the renderer applies the same authored fade, so renders are scored unscaled. The comparator measures a per-frame fade level from the captured palette; frames below `--min-fade-level` (default 0.25, ticks 1260 onward for this capture set) are reported but not scored, because SSIM of near-black frames says little. The headline is `mean_ssim_scored`, quoted with its scored-frame count; the end-hold captures are the faded tail, not a blank scene. The render's clear colour for the IoU sky mask comes from its `palette_controls.json`. Method and fields are in [`research/parity/method.md`](../research/parity/method.md#21-fade-floor). The fade itself is authored (`[Established]`): film variable 14 is palette brightness, 15 saturation and 13 a night term, interpolated per frame by `PLAYONE` and applied by the renderer ([`research/playone-exe/palette-fade.md`](../research/playone-exe/palette-fade.md), Q-FLM-09).

## 2. Camera Look Direction & Partial Model Fidelity (`CTRY.FLM`) (`[Bounded Observation]`)

* On `CTRY.FLM` tick 1 the view slot sits at `(-3306.62, -14.0, 2614.11)` m, 7.0 m from the drawn actor at `(-3313.4, -12.3, 2614.53)` m, both in DOS axes (GLOSSARY.md). The evaluator maps DOS axes to `[I1]` by a 180° rotation about Z (positions `(-x, -y, z)`, orientations `q_z180 * q`; `[Bounded Observation]`, consistent with § 3), so the evaluated eye is `(3306.62, 14.0, 2614.11)` m and every `TrajectoryRecord` is `[I1]` with no consumer-side heuristic. A camera target at the world origin is 4215 m away and behind the scene, so the view slot's BAM rotation — not an origin default — defines the look direction: `target = eye + rotate_quat(rotation_quat, [0, 0, 1]) * 1000 m`.
* The stored slot rotation maps camera space to world space with `+Z` pointing into the screen, and its pitch angle `rx` (`rot_bam[1]`) is the negative of `quat_from_euler`'s `pitch`. With both conventions applied, the tick 1 look direction lands **1.4°** off the bearing to the drawn actor; every other combination of axis sign and pitch/yaw sign tested lands 8°–178° off. Without the pitch negation the camera misses the actor entirely and every rendered frame is sky.
* `CTRY.ALL` holds 127 `MODEL_*.SOD` chunks. With the IPG subtype-4 operand width, SHADE16, and TAP decoding corrected, 126 decode to `RETURN`/EOF and only `0x0107` stops early at byte `0x16` (opcode `0x01F8`, ANIMATE). `0x0402`–`0x0408` now reach `RETURN` with non-degenerate geometry: `OP_TAP` (`0x022E`) copies `count` 28-byte anchor records into the vertex register bank, using the operand form `reg_off, count, count × anchor_byte_offsets` (`# [FMT-SOD-TAP]`, `# [MEM-SOD-ANCHOR-TABLE]`) `[Established]`. `T09` is complete; `T10` (ANIMATE) remains open in [`TEAM_SHEET.md`](../docs/TEAM_SHEET.md). The drawn actor's chunk (`shape_id 0x7F37`, 7604 bytes) now reaches `RETURN` at `0x11F2`, recovering the two facets after `0x02B5` (SHADE16; two-word operand width `[Established]`, lighting effect `[Bounded Observation]`). The parity renderer keeps the geometry decoded before any stop (`transpile_sod_lenient`) rather than discarding the model.
* **`OP_ANIMATE` (`0x01F8`) (`[Bounded Observation]`):** The saved `PLAYONE.EXE` disassembly shows a variable-length resource table and a screen-space pixel blit, not a 3D joint-angle update. `CTRY.ALL` chunk `0x0107` emits no polygons, but that alone does not establish that it has no visible effect. The `.FLM` `animate: u16` values co-occur with SOD resource tables in `Ac`, but the dataflow into the selector remains unresolved; see [`sod-animate.md`](playone-exe/sod-animate.md). T10 remains open.

## 4. Night Film Parity (`COL3-2.FLM`) (`[Bounded Observation]`)

Scored with the procedure below against the 30 frames of [`palette-fade.md` § 5.1](playone-exe/palette-fade.md#51-night-capture-col3-2flm), where the modelled palette is already exact.

* **Result:** mean SSIM 0.666, minimum 0.479, mean IoU 0.651. The four black frames (ticks 168–201) score 0.925–0.931. Every frame from tick 458 on scores 0.62–0.66 with a constant IoU of 0.751. This is far below the `[I6]` gate, and the cause is not colour.
* **What the reference shows:** from tick 458 the original draws a studio interior (an anchorman at a desk, a window with an outdoor scene in it, caption text from the `0x99` credits opcode, and the UI bar). `stuntreel` draws only the empty outdoor view toward the horizon. The film's `CNN Headline News` credits at tick 198 and its 26 created slots are consistent with a news-studio set.
* **Evaluator state at tick 458:** the active camera is slot 7 (`0x97` at tick 448) at `(6239.6, 0.30, -3247.3)` m, with look target `eye + (17, -87, 996)` m, so it points almost straight along `+Z`. The evaluator places 18 actors, which are exactly the slots 8-25 created with `0x13`/`0x17`/`0x1b` at tick 198. The near ones are `15135` (0.5 x 0.1 x 0.2 m, 0.55 m away), `16495` (4.4 x 2.0 x 5.9 m, 1.14 m), `16393` and `16423` (3-5 m blocks, 34 m) and `16421` (91 m, 372 m); eleven more are 15.6 m-tall models 4.9 km away, which are the figures on our horizon. None is a person-sized shape or a room-sized wall. Slots 2-4 are created with `0x19` at tick 194 and with `0x12` (exists bit clear) at tick 198, which destroys them. Five other shapes (`261`, `262`, `266`, `268`, `14976`) stop early at opcode `504` (`UnknownOpcode`), none of them among the placed actors.
* **Ruled out (`[Bounded Observation]`):** the near-clip plane (0.5 m to 0.05 m left the frame unchanged); terrain occlusion (rendering without `--sidata` still shows no studio); back-face culling (the pipeline has none); a mirrored model (the model-to-world matrices are proper rotations); the visibility gates (every near mesh passes them, and all 70 vertices of `15135` are in front of the camera). Pulling the camera back 3 m or 10 m draws some dark furniture-scale blocks, so the near actors do render, but not the walls, window or anchorman.
* **Where the 18 actors sit (`[Bounded Observation]`):** in three clusters, stable from tick 200 to 458. The studio cluster is at `(6240, 0, -3247)` m: shapes `15135`, `16495`, `16423`, `16393` and one copy of `16421`. A row of nine shapes (`15486`-`15530`, 12 m apart, 15.6 m tall) sits near `(1900, 8, -879)`. Three more (`15111`, `15134`, `16421`) sit near `(-3681, 1, 342)`. The camera is in the studio cluster. No shape there is person-sized or room-sized: the largest near one is `16495` (4.4 x 2.0 x 5.9 m, 52 vertices, 20 + 2 + 4 triangles in three meshes).
* **Frame composition in `PLAYONE` (`[Supported Inference]`, static):** the per-frame routine at `0x0EE9` (entered from `0x4A35` with `BX = 0x4888`, the snapshot copy of the film state made at `0x39B0`) builds the sun vector (`0x196C`), sets the view matrix (`0x11FC`), makes one 3D scene draw through `0x824:0x0A05`, then draws the credits string at `+0xFE` with `0x58BD`. There is no second view or window pass: `DS:0x2275`, which selects `0x824:0x07DC` with `AX = 0xE8`, `CX = 0xD0`, is a video-mode flag set next to an `int 10h` mode change (`0x6E1B`, `0x737B`). So the window in the reference is part of the one scene, not a composited inset.
* **Conclusion:** the studio is not among the shapes the evaluator places near the camera, and `PLAYONE` has no extra layer to supply it, so the original must place or build something we do not. Candidates, none tested: the anchorman and walls are slots whose shapes decode to far less geometry than the original draws (for example a model composed of references that the display-list VM does not follow); `PLAYONE` places or scales slots differently from the evaluator; or the set is drawn from data absent from the `.ALL` and the staging mount. `COL3-2.ALL` holds no `0x8100`-`0xBFFF` scenery chunk, only 750 models, one palette and sounds. Vars 2-7, 10 and 11 change at tick 198 (see [`palette-fade.md` § 3.2](playone-exe/palette-fade.md#32-other-readers-of-the-live-variables)); none has a reader that explains the studio.
* **Display-list decode is not lossy for the studio shapes (`[Bounded Observation]`):** `16495`, `15135`, `16423`, `16393`, `16421`, `15111` and `15486` use only `POLYZ`, `COLOR`, `IFVIS`, `IPG` type 0 (a cube generator), vertex arithmetic and a single `PERS`. None uses `SPHERE`, `BLOCK`, `PERFDISK` or `IPG` type 4 (subobject), the opcodes whose geometry `vm.rs` reads and discards. `16495` has 13 `POLYZ` ops and decodes to 26 triangles, so the missing studio is not dropped geometry.
* **Shape census (`[Bounded Observation]`):** up to tick 470 the film's `create` opcodes bind only shape ids in `0x3B07`-`0x4027`, `0x3C7E`-`0x3CAA`, `0x3E57` and `0x406F`. There is no person or character id; slots 0-7 are rigs without the draw bit. The anchorman is therefore not a shape this film binds to a slot, yet the original draws one.
* **Deferred (open):** read `PLAYONE`'s own slot and shape tables at runtime around tick 458 (the `0x4778` / `0x48B6` arrays and the shape each active slot resolves to) with the capture harness's debugger, and compare with our 18 actors. That separates "the evaluator drops or misplaces slots" from "the engine draws something the film does not list".
* **The scorer misfires at night:** the fade level in `report.json` is a brightness heuristic built for the `CTRY.FLM` day sky. At night it reads about 0.24 for most frames, so 29 of 30 are excluded and the scored headline rests on one frame (tick 705, fade 1.012, SSIM 0.479). Read the unscored mean for this film.

**Procedure for scoring another captured film.** `--frames a..b` is 1-based, so `--frames 459..460` writes `tick_00458.png`; render the whole captured span (`--frames 160..730` for this capture), because frames outside the range count as "missing on one side". Render at `--width 320 --height 200`, since the parity command rejects 320x240 input. Then run `parity --reference build/parity/reference/<film> --test build/parity/ours/<film> --output build/parity/report/<film>`. The render directory must hold the `.idx`/`.layer` planes `--headless` writes beside each frame. For a side-by-side look, decode a reference `.idx` page (64000 bytes, 320x200) with its `palettes_NNNN.json` and paste it beside our PNG; the `contact_*.png` sheet only shows the first frame.

## 5. Loss Attribution (`CTRY.FLM`) (`[Bounded Observation]`)

2026-10-22, one render (viewport 185 rows), one capture set (`ctry`), 114 scored frames, `mean_ssim_scored` 0.689. `stuntreel parity` splits each SSIM window's loss (`1 - ssim`) among the render layers of its pixels in proportion to pixel count (`mean_loss_by_class_scored` in `report.json`); the classes sum to `1 - mean_ssim_scored` = 0.311.

| Class (what **our render** draws there) | Loss | Share |
|---|---|---|
| props (`SCENERY.scn` placements) | 0.145 | 47% |
| terrain (`MAP.BIN` patches) | 0.121 | 39% |
| reference-only (capture non-sky, render sky) | 0.035 | 11% |
| actors | 0.008 | 3% |
| sky, ocean | 0.003 | 1% |
| render-only (render draws where the capture is sky) | 0.000 | 0% |

* **Both layers have geometry where the capture does, but the shading is wrong.** Render-only loss is 0 and reference-only is 11%, so coverage is largely right (consistent with mask IoU 0.853); the bulk of the loss is how the covered ground and props are shaded and textured. Median per-frame loss is 0.114 for props and 0.114 for terrain.
* **Attribution is by what our render draws, not what is missing.** In the worst props frame (ordinal 41, tick 448, SSIM 0.515) the capture shows the hero biplane (checkered wings) and its ground shadow, while our render shows the aircraft as faint struts and no shadow over a flat dark ground. The loss lands on the ground class (props 0.35) because the render draws ground, not an actor, there. The actors class (0.008) therefore **understates** the aircraft's cost. The drawn actor's shape `0x7F37` decodes to `RETURN`, so the thin render is not an unimplemented opcode; cause not identified (candidates: state mask or LOD selecting too little geometry, winding or palette indices). Ground shadow: not drawn at all; no code or finding yet.
* **Terrain is worst on `ordinal 52`/`55`/`35` (ticks 568, 601, 382), 0.22 to 0.25 each**; not yet inspected.
* The remaining partial model is `0x0107` (`OP_ANIMATE`, `0x01F8`, T10); it is the only chunk that stops early, and its cost is not isolated by this split.
* **Method limits.** Layer ids: 1 ocean, 2 terrain, 3 props, 16+n actor draw n of the frame (draw order, not film slot). The index plane is unlit, so the split says where loss is, not that the palette index is wrong. Windows touching the UI overlay are excluded.

Next probes, ranked by attributed loss: (1) why the hero aircraft renders thin and why it has no shadow; (2) terrain shading versus the capture on ticks 382, 568, 601; (3) prop shading and detail.

## 6. Directional Lighting Is Wrong; Unlit Palette Colours Match (`CTRY.FLM`) (`[Bounded Observation]`)

2026-10-22, `CTRY.FLM`, four capture sets, 114 scored frames (viewport 185 rows).

* **Evidence.** In capture ordinal 41 (tick 448) the airfield ground is palette index 196, `(69,121,45)`, over 43k of the 59k viewport pixels. Our render drew the same index at `(28,49,18)`, 41% brightness, because the shader chose a lighting row from `ambient + (1 - ambient) * max(n . sun, 0)` with `sun = (0.3, 0.9, 0.3)` and `ambient = 0.25`; many polygons evidently have normals pointing away from that sun.
* **Experiments** (`mean_ssim_scored`, capture set `ctry`): baseline lit 0.689; two-sided lit (`abs(n . sun)`) 0.764; constant lighting row 31 (full brightness, i.e. the palette colour as is) **0.766**; constant row 26 0.760, row 22 0.745, row 16 0.700. Full brightness is best and any ambient dimming is worse, so the capture shows polygons at their palette index, not at a sun-derived level. Over all four capture sets the unlit render scores 0.766, 0.767, 0.767, 0.766 (spread 0.001).
* **Change.** `mode13h.wgsl` now draws every fragment at lighting row 31. The camera's `sun_direction` and `ambient_intensity` uniforms are still uploaded but unused. The original's per-polygon shading is explicit (`OP_SHADES`, `OP_SHADE16`, per-vertex shade offsets in `GVPOLY`), not a sun vector, so a faithful shading model is still open; the capture's ground ramp indices (for example 162 and 163 beside 196) are evidence for it.
* **Loss attribution after the change** (replaces the § 5 figures): terrain 0.116, props 0.072, reference-only 0.035, actors 0.008, sky and ocean 0.003, total 0.234.
* **Hero aircraft (`0x7F37`), still open.** It decodes to 36 polygons (20 `POLYZ`, 16 `GVPOLY`), 132 vertices, `ground_y_offset` 2624 at scale index 13 (0.32 m). Compared with the capture at tick 448: the wingspan matches (113 px against 101 px) but our aircraft sits about 13 rows lower (rows 90 to 121 against 76 to 106, a diagonal-wing pattern in both); the capture's wings alternate blue (index 136) with white (63 and neighbours) stripes and its fuselage is white, while ours is index 136 and 47 only, so some striped or white polygons are missing. A camera pitch bias of -0.5 to -4 degrees and a hero-only ground-offset multiplier of -2 to 4 did not give a consistent score gain (offset 0 gave +0.005, pitch -2 gave +0.002 with IoU 0.853 to 0.880); neither is adopted. `[Unknown]`: why the stripes are missing and why the aircraft is lower.
* **Ground shadow, still open.** The capture draws a black monoplane-shaped silhouette on the ground below the aircraft (the aircraft itself is a biplane, and CTRY has only one actor draw at tick 448). Nothing in our renderer draws it; its source (a flattened projection by `PLAYONE`, an `OP_ANIMATE` image resource, or something else) is `[Unknown]`.
* **Sky gradient: closed in § 7.**

## 7. Sky Is Elevation Bands of Palette Indices 228 to 238 (`CTRY.FLM`) (`[Bounded Observation]`)

2026-10-22, `CTRY.FLM`, capture set `ctry`, 114 scored frames.

* **Observation.** The capture's sky is not one colour. It is straight, parallel bands (tilted with the camera roll) of palette indices 238 at the horizon through 228 high up, 11 bands, and each band's pixels sit at a stable view-ray elevation. With our camera (`eye`, `target`, `roll_radians`, `fov_degrees` per tick, now written to `palette_controls.json` as `camera`) the elevation of the band transitions has medians of 2.14, 2.77, 3.59, 4.61, 5.82, 7.27, 9.23, 11.79, 15.75 and 20.23 degrees (1,200 to 1,600 transitions per edge, interquartile range about 0.5 to 1.3 degrees). Successive edges grow by a ratio of about 1.28 (1.25 to 1.34), so the bands are roughly geometric in elevation. Index 228 holds up to the highest elevation seen (23.4 degrees); behaviour above that, and indices 224 to 227, are `[Unknown]`. Below the first edge, including below the horizon where nothing is drawn, index 238.
* **Camera accuracy limits the fit.** A pixel-exact classifier with a fixed geometric law reaches only about 65 to 75% per frame, and a per-frame pitch shift of up to 1.4 degrees (mean 0.05, standard deviation 0.95) improves it little, so the residual comes from our camera's per-tick error, not from the band law. The edge medians are therefore the empirical table, not an exact rule.
* **Change.** The renderer paints the sky first in each pass (`vs_sky`, `fs_sky`, `fs_sky_plane` in `mode13h.wgsl`): a fullscreen triangle that turns each pixel into a world ray through `CameraUniform::inv_view_proj`, takes the sine of its elevation and picks the band index from the table above, with layer 0 (none) in the plane target. `CameraUniform` grows to 160 bytes.
* **Effect** (capture set `ctry`): `mean_ssim_scored` 0.766 to 0.769; mean index agreement 0.390 to 0.470 (the sky now carries the right indices); mask IoU unchanged at 0.853.
* **Index 227 is a sky band too** (2026-10-23, all three capture sets): `CTRY.FLM`'s capture never uses 227 (228 to 238 only), but 27 of 213 `INTRO.FLM` frames (32,194 pixels) and 4 of 30 `COL3-2.FLM` frames (1,284 pixels) do, above 228. Parity therefore treats 227 to 238 as sky (`CAPTURE_SKY_INDICES` in `crates/stunt-cli/src/parity/mask.rs`; see § 12). Whether 227 is the top band of the same law or a separate colour is `[Unknown]`; the renderer's band table is unchanged.

## 8. Camera Roll Wrapped the Long Way (`CTRY.FLM`) (`[Established]` as a defect, `[Bounded Observation]` for the effect)

2026-10-22. The camera roll between two timeline records was interpolated as a plain scalar. `CTRY.FLM` moves the view slot's roll across the 0 / 2π wrap (about 0 degrees at tick 20, about 359.5 degrees at tick 43), so ticks in between were rendered with a roll of up to 294.5 degrees (tick 32) instead of about 0. The capture shows a near-level horizon there. Roll now interpolates along the shorter arc (`lerp_angle` in `crates/stunt-core/src/flm/interpolate.rs`); roll reads 0, then 360, then 359 degrees across ticks 20 to 44. Capture ordinal 3 (tick 32) moved from SSIM 0.66 to 0.78; `mean_ssim_scored` over 114 frames 0.769 to 0.770.

* **Shadow experiment (not adopted).** A black copy of each actor's geometry flattened onto a horizontal plane (swept at -1, 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 6 and 10 m, layer and forced palette index 0) lowered `mean_ssim_scored` from 0.769 to 0.762 to 0.766 at every height, so the capture's ground shadow is not a flat copy of the aircraft at a guessed constant ground height. The capture's shadow is a silhouette roughly 60 rows below the aircraft in ordinal 41 and its outline is monoplane-like. Its construction is still `[Unknown]`.

## 10. Tree Crowns Are `OP_SPHERE`; Ground Shade Is World-Fixed (`CTRY.FLM`) (`[Bounded Observation]`)

2026-10-22, `CTRY.FLM` capture set `ctry`, 114 scored frames. Method: contact sheets, then enlarged crops of capture against render (ordinal 100, tick 1095), then a per-pixel join of the capture index with the render's layer plane.

* **Trees had trunks and no crowns.** In the capture the horizon trees are large round green clumps; our render showed only thin trunks. ## 13. Where the Loss Actually Is: Edges, Not Flat Colour (`CTRY.FLM`, `INTRO.FLM`) (`[Bounded Observation]`)

2026-10-23, `main` at `d90a047`, both films re-rendered and re-scored on the current renderer (no DBG switches; the two sweeps below used a temporary switch that was reverted). CTRY 0.7708 over 114 scored frames (index agreement 0.475, IoU 0.978); INTRO 0.6762 over 209 (0.481, 0.919). Loss by class: CTRY terrain 0.113, props 0.074, sky 0.018, reference-only 0.014, actors 0.008; INTRO terrain 0.170, props 0.068, reference-only 0.029, sky 0.024, ocean 0.012.

* **The loss is structural.** Recomputing the comparator's 11x11/stride-8 windows in Python over the 93 CTRY frames above the fade that still use the full palette: 70% of the total window loss sits in windows whose capture luminance standard deviation is above 10, 17% in flat windows (sd < 2) and 12% in between. Terrain-major windows (80% or more terrain pixels) are 55% of all windows and carry 39% of the loss, of which 75% is at edges. Windows touching an actor carry 13%. A uniform colour error therefore explains little; the horizon, hills, prop silhouettes, coastline, shadow and the aircraft account for nearly all of it. (The absolute SSIM differs from `report.json` because this run uses the index plane and the static palette rather than the PNG, so read the shares, not the mean.)
* **One polygon carries three shades, so the shade is spatial.** A temporary `DBG_TERRAIN_ID` drew every terrain patch at its own palette index, and the render's `.idx` plane was joined to the capture through the layer plane over all 114 scored frames. `MAP.BIN` patch 4 alone holds 2,438,973 of the 3.35 million terrain pixels (73%) and the capture draws it at 196 for 58.3%, 194 for 24.3%, 193 for 15.4% and 195 for only 0.3% (ramp pixels only; the rest are props and edge pixels). The shade therefore varies *inside* a single flat polygon: it is not a property of the patch, and no per-patch lookup can reproduce it. All 12 colour-195 patches are flat (raw y is 0) and none is concave, and fan triangulation never spills (fan area equals polygon area for all 166 active patches), so the variation is not geometry either.
* **`f[10]` cannot be the shade driver.** Patch 4's five anchors all carry `f[10] = 31`, and so do patches 1, 7, 25 and 28, so an interpolated `f[10]` would be constant across the very polygon that shows three shades. Across the island `f[10]` is 0 for 826 of 1,024 anchors and 2 to 31 for the rest, and the airfield patches that do differ (patch 10: 31,31,29,13,13; patch 33: 11,2,27,27,31) are the far ones. The earlier suggestion that `f[10]` selects the ground shade is withdrawn.
* **Coplanar terrain patches are not the cause.** Drawing base geometry with `LessEqual` instead of `Less` (a painter-like tie-break for the y=0 patches) leaves both films' scores identical to four decimals (0.7706 / 0.6762 both ways, IoU unchanged), so no two terrain patches reach the depth test at equal depth and the sand/airfield overlap is not an order tie. A temporary `DBG_DEPTH_LE` made the check and was reverted.
* **A simple distance cue is not the law.** A temporary `DBG_TERRAIN_CUE` drew terrain one palette step brighter inside a view-depth threshold and one step darker outside (`mean_ssim_scored`, CTRY / INTRO): off 0.7708 / 0.6762; 15 m 0.7574 / 0.6667; 25 m 0.7572 / 0.6677; 40 m 0.7586 / 0.6689; 80 m 0.7625 / 0.6689. Restricting the cue to the green ramp (indices 192 to 199) recovers part of the loss (0.7601 / 0.6727 at 15 m) but still loses on both films. The single-frame fit that suggested the rule (tick 273 over patch 4: +1.00 at 10 to 19 m, -0.72 at 30 to 39 m, -1.00 beyond 40 m) does not generalise: over 114 frames the 0 to 19 m bin averages +0.36. The shade is not a fixed-distance depth cue.
* **Method warning for depth effects in `mode13h.wgsl`.** The cue was first computed as `length(world_pos - camera_pos)`, which read about 4 km for every terrain fragment: Euclidean distance is not affine in world space, so perspective-correct interpolation across a kilometre-sized terrain triangle is invalid. `(view_proj * world_pos).w` is the affine view depth and interpolates exactly; any depth effect in this shader should use it.
* **Uniform terrain offsets are rejected.** `DBG_TERRAIN_SHADE` added a constant to every terrain palette index (`mean_ssim_scored`, CTRY / INTRO): -1 -> 0.7643 / 0.6725, 0 -> **0.7708 / 0.6762**, +1 -> 0.7683 / 0.6675, +2 -> 0.7548 / 0.6506. The 0.7% of colour-195 pixels the capture leaves at 195 and the 15% at 193 are enough that one offset costs both films; nothing was adopted.
* **A camera pitch bias is CTRY-only.** A temporary `DBG_CAM_PITCH` rotated the authored view about its right axis (`mean_ssim_scored`, CTRY / INTRO): -2 degrees 0.7598 / 0.6542, -1.5 0.7696 / 0.6601, -1 0.7746 / 0.6620, -0.5 0.7784 / 0.6672, 0 0.7718 / 0.6754, +0.5 0.7566 / 0.6682, +1 0.7475 / 0.6618. CTRY peaks at -0.5 degrees (+0.007) while INTRO peaks at 0, the same CTRY-only signature as the eye-height and hero-lift sweeps of § 11. The camera knobs raise the CTRY scene only by moving the whole world, which is why they cost INTRO; the residual is consistent with the CTRY hero sitting about 10 px low (§ 11), not with a camera error. Nothing was adopted.
* **Render determinism.** Two separate processes rendering ticks 589 and 590 produced byte-identical `.idx` planes, so renders are reproducible; the same configuration scored 0.7706, 0.7708 and 0.7718 across three different builds, so treat differences under about 0.002 between builds as floating-point noise rather than a result.
* **The original's terrain drawing is not in the data.** `corpus/.../FreeFlight/src/sisland.cpp` (the community's reimplementation) draws every `MAP_AREA` as one flat `glColor3f(palette[color])` polygon and never reads any `f[]` scalar; `simap.cpp` only walks the scenery tree. So the per-region shade is produced by `PLAYONE.EXE`'s own renderer, not selected by map or anchor data, which is why matching it needs the player's draw path rather than a data field. The only per-vertex field that varies across patch 4 at all is `f[8]` (1, 8, 102, 115, 116 on its five anchors; the other 1,024 anchors spread over -23 to 145 with 826 zeros), and its meaning is still `[Unknown]`.
* **The actor's horizontal offset is not a tick lag.** A temporary `DBG_ACTOR_TICKS` sampled only the actors at `tick + d` while the camera stayed at `tick` (`mean_ssim_scored`, CTRY / INTRO): -6 -> 0.7645 / 0.6777, -4 -> 0.7677 / 0.6750, -2 -> 0.7705 / 0.6751, 0 -> **0.7708 / 0.6762**, +2 -> 0.7685 / 0.6753, +4 -> 0.7652 / 0.6747, +6 -> 0.7617 / 0.6744. Triangles by palette index: 0 -> 17, 47 -> 10, 56 -> 104, 74 -> 24, 136 -> 40. ## 18. Why the Ground Ramp Cannot Come From Our Data: PLAYONE Gouraud-Shades The Index (`CTRY.FLM`, `INTRO.FLM`) (`[Established]` as a mechanism)

2026-10-23, `integration/library-adoption` after `48a7576`. Sections 15-17 closed the data-side explanations for the flat ground's shade; a delegated capstone disassembly of the unpacked `PLAYONE.EXE` then found the mechanism. Details, instruction listings and address table: [`playone-exe/terrain-shade.md`](playone-exe/terrain-shade.md).

* **The ramp is in palette-index space, in screen space.** Each ground polygon is filled by a Gouraud ramp on the palette index itself in 8.8 fixed point (`0x75DB`; per-pixel `stosb` plus a 16-bit accumulate at `0x7671`). Per vertex `s = -clamp((N . L) >> 16, +/-0x3FFF)` from the `SHADES` opcode (`0x9481`), then `idx = max(0, highbyte(s >> 1) + G) + base` and the vertex is stored as `(idx << 8) | lowbyte(s >> 1)`. There is no per-pixel depth term and no depth buffer.
* **This is the measured behaviour.** Iso-index lines are straight in *screen* space, so a hard boundary appears inside one flat polygon; on pitched ground an iso-line of constant screen row is roughly a constant world-z line (the "18-20 m" boundary of section 15) but it is not a world plane, which is why the plane fit and the per-patch lookups of sections 13-17 all failed. Two to four adjacent ramp entries per polygon, hard edges, never the data colour: exactly what the captures show.
* **No constant offset anywhere.** The global one-step offset `G` is 0 in the shipped build (`0x0E1C` builds it from `DGROUP:0x713`, which is `0xFF`), so the whole "one entry away" has to come from the light term, not from a build flag.
* **The colour is not `MAP.BIN`'s.** This binary never opens `MAP.BIN` (no name literal, and the 30-byte stride immediate `0x1E` does not occur anywhere in `0x0000..0xD510`); the base colour is one operand byte of a ground display list (`0x9988`) and the demo build embeds or streams its own ground geometry. So the per-vertex normals the player shades with are not the `MAP.BIN` patch records or their anchors, which is why section 17's attribution to record 4 found three shades inside one patch: patch 4 is not one polygon to PLAYONE.
* **Two inputs are still missing for an implementation.** (1) The per-shot light vector `L` (`work:0x8072/4/6`): its only writer is `0x9106` inside the per-frame setup `0x9100`, and that entry is reached indirectly - no direct `call`/`jmp`/`lcall`/`mov` in the image references it. (2) PLAYONE's ground display list (polygons with their colour byte, vertex registers and `SHADES` normal blocks), which has to be located and decoded.
* **Next probes.** Breakpoint `0x9106` at the post-retrace trap in DOSBox-X, dump `AX/BX/DX` and the return address across two differently shaded shots (film ticks 10 and 448), compare with `DS:0x49EC` and the var-13 value; then locate the embedded ground display list in the image or in the LOAD FILM-dialog memory dumps.
* **Status.** No renderer change was adopted; this explains the largest single levers (the shade oracle of section 16 is +0.0105 CTRY / +0.0029 INTRO) and turns the next round into a data-recovery job rather than a fitting exercise.

## 19. The Light Only Brightens, So The Ground's Base Colours Are Baked (`CTRY.FLM`) (`[Bounded Observation]` + `[Supported Inference]`)

2026-10-23, `integration/library-adoption` at `b44c6bc`. Section 18 recovered the shading mechanism statically; this round measured its inputs live and drew the consequence for our data.

* **Tool.** New [`python/scripts/probe_playone_light.py`](../python/scripts/probe_playone_light.py) drives one film through the DOSBox-X TAC harness (the same control topology as `mise run capture-ref`) and, at chosen film clocks, reads the display-list work segment (its far pointer is at `DS:0x0542`; runtime value `0x1DF8`) at `0x8072` (light vector), `0x8078` (sun billboard), `0x7468` (first shade-array words) and `DS:0x49EC` (sun position), then dumps both 60 KiB segments for offline analysis (`build/scratch/round5/`, gitignored).
* **Measured.** The light vector is a unit vector at 1.0 = 32767 (magnitudes 32761-32766): `(99, 32766, -17)` at film clocks 14, 65, 448, 702, 900, 1100 and 1251 (straight up), tilted at 155 `(-19344, 26134, -4016)`, 276 `(-15780, 25000, -14120)`, 345 `(-9156, 28402, -13524)` and 564 `(-570, 31498, -8998)`. The sun-billboard vector at `0x8078` is exactly `-L`, and `DS:0x49EC` holds `L` in its first three words. The shade-array words change with the tilt (they are negative at clock 564), so the harness reads a real per-frame lighting state.
* **The composition can only brighten.** `idx = base + max(0, highbyte(s >> 1) + G)` with `G = 0` (section 18), so a polygon is drawn at its base colour or brighter, never darker. The captures draw the airfield region at 193, 194, 195 and 196 - values both above and below our `MAP.BIN` colour 195, with 193 on about 90% of flat pixels in the late frames. One base colour cannot produce both directions, so **PLAYONE's ground display list carries its own per-polygon base colours** (a bake of the island), and our `MAP.BIN` patch colour 195 is simply not the byte the player uses (the byte is read from the display list at `0x9988`).
* **Reading.** The "per-shot ramp level" of sections 15-17 is mostly this baked base colour, not a light level: the light adds a small non-negative offset on top of a polygon colour that we never see. That explains why no per-frame light fit ever worked and why the same view 33 m apart showed different shades: it is a different set of player polygons, each with its own baked colour.
* **Next.** Decode the ground display list from the dumped segments (or a fresh LOAD FILM-dialog dump): polygon records with their colour byte, vertex registers and `SHADES` normal blocks. Comparing its base colours with `MAP.BIN`'s patch colours decides the fix: (a) re-bake our terrain colours from the player's bake rule, or (b) build the terrain from the player's display list directly. The tool above makes both a dump-and-decode job rather than a fitting exercise.

## 22. The Ground Record Layout, and No Resident Display List (`CTRY.FLM`) (`[Bounded Observation]`)

2026-10-23, `integration/library-adoption` at `39a3868`, delegated decode. This corrects the "baked display list" framing of sections 19-21: the record layout is now known, but no such list was found resident in the player at any clock, so the premise that the player carries a static baked colour list is unproven.

* **The record layout.** Handler `0x9982` reads `ch = es:[si+1]`, `bx = es:[si+2]`; the shared tail at `0x996E` does `lodsw; and ax, 0xF; shl ax, 1; add si, ax; lodsw; jmp ax`. So a ground record is `w0 = (colour << 8) | (vertex_count & 0x0F)` with `count <= 15`, then `N` words indexing `work:0x7468` (the per-vertex `s` written by `SHADES`), then the next code address: the stream is threaded. Colour and count share one word, which is why the earlier structural scans (separate colour byte, shade word and count) could never match.
* **No resident list.** Rescanning the image and the round-5/6 memory dumps with the corrected layout (colour 192-205, count 3-8, shade words below `0x1000`, next word a valid opcode) gives one chance hit in the image and no run of records: the ground display list is not in conventional memory at clocks 276 or 277.
* **Where the world data can come from.** The demo install has no `MAP.BIN` and no `ANCHORPT.DAT`; the player's only file literals are `game.cfg/var`, `pfont.bin`, `pilots`, `res\*.res` (+ sound formats), `driver1..3.bin`, `vdata.bin` and `VAULT\*.FLM/.TKE`. World data therefore comes from the `res\*.res` cache (payloads look packed) or from the image. Candidate embedded regions: image `0x23200-0x24E80` (dense 9k-opcode field with `0x20A`/`0x213`/`0x225`/`0x22E`, best match for a serialised scenery program), `0x2D500-0x2F000` (10-12-byte nodes with forward links that are not image addresses, consistent with a template relocated by the loader), and `0x2AC00-0x2B000` (prop bytecode template). None decodes as a ground polygon list.
* **Revised reading.** The additive-only argument still holds (the light can only brighten), so the captures' 193/194/196 around our 195 still cannot come from one base. But the base bytes may be *computed at load* rather than stored: the image carries `OP_FRACTAL` (`0x1D4`) and `OP_IPG` (Ground), i.e. terrain could be generated with a computed per-polygon colour. That would explain both the per-polygon variation and the absence of any static list.
* **Next probe (one harness run).** Break at `0x9982`/`0x99A6` (or trace the filler `0x75DB`) at the post-retrace trap and recover `ES:SI` and `CS:IP` for the first ground polygon; if `ES` points at a record stream, decode it with the layout above and disassemble the builder behind it. If the handler is never reached for ground pixels, the terrain path is a dedicated routine and the baked-list premise should be dropped in favour of the generated-terrain route. Cheap pre-check: dump the EMS page frame (EMMXXXX0 is present) and grep it for `colour << 8 | count` headers with colours 192-199.

## 23. The Ground Colour Is Stored Per Polygon, But The Builder Is Still Unknown (`CTRY.FLM`) (`[Established]` for the chain, `[Unknown]` for the producer)

2026-10-23, `integration/library-adoption` at `728b9ab`, delegated static trace. It settles what the colour byte is (stored per polygon) and rules out every place the list could have been resident.

* **The chain.** Ground handler `0x995C` (op `0x168`) tests `work:0x806C/0x806B`, then the simple path at `0x9982` or the clipped path `0x99A6`; `0x9A24` (op `0x2BE`) is the same at `0x9A43`. The colour is `mov ch, es:[si+1]` (`0x9988`, only other site `0x9A50`): the high byte of the record word `w0 = (colour << 8) | (count & 0x0F)`. The words after `w0` index `work:0x7468` (`0x99E1`/`0x99E5`), the per-vertex `s` written by `SHADES`; `0x99E9` halves it, `0x99EB` adds `G = 0`, `0x99F3` adds the base, `0x99F5` stores the 8.8 index. The filler is `lcall 0,0x75DB` at `0x9A15` (other ground ops `0x9AE5`, `0x9B33`).
* **No palette lookup, no table, no arithmetic on the base.** The byte is consumed directly; the only other site that sets the fill colour (`OP_COLOR`, op `0x129` -> `0x975A`, `work:0x41E8`) is not read on this path.
* **The stream is self-patching threaded code.** The handler tail (`0x996E`) is `lodsw; and ax, 0xF; shl ax, 1; add si, ax; lodsw; jmp ax`, so the word after the shade words is a CS:IP. Handlers write their own continuation into the stream (`0x92CD` op `0x15F` writes `0x1241`, `0x93DD` likewise) and every dispatch slot carries `26 C7 44 FE imm16` after its jump, so a first pass must translate opcodes into those continuations.
* **The list is generated at runtime and gone at the probe point.** No `0x9481`/`0x995C` word in the image; no `(colour 0xC0-0xCD, count 3-8)` record with a valid next-IP; and none of the 72 memory dumps (rounds 5, 6, `build/re/mem`) contains handler IPs (`0x2551`, `0x2A2C`), slot aliases (`0x146C`, `0x147E`, `0x15D4`) or opcode words. The earlier searches only looked for opcodes and colours, so this is the first search that could have found a threaded stream.
* **Possible producers.** `OP_IPG` (op `0x0063` -> `0x965F`, type dispatcher via `DS:0x1954`, type 2 = ground quad, geometry only) and `OP_FRACTAL` (op `0x01D4` -> `0xA56C`, builds a four-point ground polygon from pushed vertices and writes `work:0x41E8` from its stack frame at `0xA665`) can both emit terrain records with a supplied colour. The two 16-bit template regions in the image (`0x23200-0x24E80`, `0x25xxx`) are loader-relocated, not a colour map; there is no dense 192-199 byte map anywhere.
* **Next probes.** Find the first-pass dispatcher - the only code that reads the slot immediates - by searching for a walker over `table + op + 5` (or an entry at `slot + 3`) and for writes of `0x2551`-style IPs into the work segment; there is no literal `0x1316` in the image, so look for the self-relative base pattern (`call $+3` / `pop si`) at `0x7B76`, `0x15D45`, `0x163A1`, `0x163BD`, `0x17148` and `0x17329`. One cheap harness cross-check: dump `work:0x1DF8` and grep it for words in `0x1F00-0x3000` (handler IPs) or `0x1400-0x1A00` (slot aliases).

## 26. `SP_1NODE` Draws A Far Stand-In Instead Of Its Subtree (`CTRY.FLM`, `INTRO.FLM`) (`[Established]` for the engine, `[Bounded Observation]` for the score)

* **Engine.** The `SP_1NODE` handler at image `0x2121` descends to `next` when the node is near and otherwise draws `obj` in place of the whole subtree ([`playone-exe/lod.md` section 1.1](playone-exe/lod.md)). `world.rs` used to skip `obj` and always descend, so every prop was drawn at its finest level of detail. `si-free-flight-2` draws both, and FreeFlight's `simap.cpp` draws neither beyond the split.

Distance is Euclidean distance to the camera polyline, where PLAYONE uses per-frame view-space depth.
* **Sweep** (`DBG_LOD_SCALE`, both films, one build): always near (old behaviour) 0.7732 / 0.6799; scale 1.0 0.7730 / 0.6834; 0.75 0.7730 / 0.6842; 0.5 0.7733 / 0.6856 (CTRY / INTRO). INTRO gains 0.0057 and CTRY moves 0.0001, which passes the adoption rule. The winner's pixels were not inspected.
* **Next.** Model `0x229C` per shot from the film variable read at `0x1200`, and use view-space depth per frame. That needs per-frame selection in the renderer, not a static world.
