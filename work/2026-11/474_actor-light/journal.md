# Journal: the actor light (#474)

## 2026-11-16: The actor light is R · L, live

- **Question**: does `PLAYONE` light a rotated actor with `R · L`, as its static read of `0x1D36` → `0824:071D` says, or with the object-frame `Rᵀ · L`, through a step not yet read (the `SHADES` normals, the rotation op at `0xA8BC`)? Open question 3 of [`terrain-shade.md`](../../../research/playone-exe/terrain-shade.md).
- **Method**: plan `tools/dosbox-x/plans/actor-light.toml` on `CTRY.FLM`, unpatched build, DOSBox-X 2026.10.01, armed at film tick 150, on `4d0902c9` (branch `issue-474`). Breaks: `0x1D20` (slot angle words in `BX`, `BP`, `SI`), `0x1D49` (the `071D` result in `DX`, `BP`, `DI`; `ES:0x80D2`, `0x80C0`, `0x808A`; `DS:0x49EC`), `0x1D8D` (the object segment in `ES`) and `0x948C`, the `OP_SHADES` loop head (`work:0x8072` read at linear `0x25FF2`, since a literal `mem:DS:` offset reads DGROUP, and the normal at `ES:SI`). Two runs: `actor-light-unpatched-20261116T103748Z.jsonl` (the first two breaks) and `-20261116T104125Z.jsonl` (all four), under `<data root>/debug/CTRY/`; a run between them, `-20261116T103921Z.jsonl`, read `work:0x8072` through a literal `mem:DS:` offset, which reads DGROUP, and is not used. A scratch script compared the doubled result with `R · L` and `Rᵀ · L` computed from the captured `R` and world light, and the captured `R` with `Ry(-w2) Rx(w1) Rz(w0)` from the angle words.
- **Result**: the one object on the `0x1CFB` path is the biplane (actor 0, shape `0x7F37`, object segment `0x5F39`), pitched about 37 degrees nose down (angle words `0xE5CB`, `0x000E`, `0x88D2` at tick 166). On 12 hits, ticks 166 to 195, the handed-on light equals `R · L` to within 2 per component; `Rᵀ · L` is off by up to about 6,600 in `z` (tick 166: handed on `(-19300, 26134, -4224)`, `R · L` `(-19299, 26136, -4223)`, `Rᵀ · L` `(-19752, 26140, 38)`). `R` matches the builder formula to within 0.0017 per entry and `0x808A` equals `V · R` exactly. In the second run all 200 `SHADES` hits (ticks 176 to 186) are the biplane's; its first list in each of five frames uses a `work:0x8072` equal word for word to that frame's handed-on light, and a second list uses it turned about `Z` (`z` kept), a sub-model's `ROTZ`. Our evaluator's slot rotation through `ShadeLight::for_actor` gives `(-19267, 26159, -4257)`, `(-19225, 26069, -4944)` and `(-19426, 25699, -5985)` at 0-based ticks 165, 174 and 185, against the handed-on `(-19300, 26134, -4224)`, `(-19260, 26044, -4912)` and `(-19408, 25736, -5864)` at film ticks 166, 175 and 186.
- **Verdict**: settled, `[Bounded Observation]`: `PLAYONE` lights a rotated actor with `R · L`. `Rᵀ · L`, and a later step that undoes the turn for the actor's body, are ruled out on this film.
- **Lives in**: [`terrain-shade.md`](../../../research/playone-exe/terrain-shade.md) section 4.3, open question 3 narrowed to the sub-model turn and the stored-matrix path.

## 2026-11-16: Lighting actors by R · L

- **Question**: does lighting each actor's shape by `R · L`, the rule of [`terrain-shade.md`](../../../research/playone-exe/terrain-shade.md) section 4.3 (image `0x1D44`, `0824:071D`), raise the reference films over the world light every actor had?
- **Method**: evidence track, no target frames. `FilmScene::frame` sets `ActorDraw::light` per actor and the renderer decodes and uploads the shape again under it; switch [`DBG_ACTOR_LIGHT`](switches/DBG_ACTOR_LIGHT.diff): 0 the world light (the old behaviour), 1 `R · L`, 2 `Rᵀ · L` as a control. `mise run sweep -- --var DBG_ACTOR_LIGHT=0,1,2 --track evidence`, on the GPU, from `4d0902c9` plus the change.
- **Result**: `mean_ssim_scored`, 0 / 1 / 2: CTRY 0.9185 / 0.9186 / 0.9185, INTRO 0.8535 / 0.8597 / 0.8567, LAND 0.8932 in all three, BALLOON 0.8174 / 0.8175 / 0.8165, TRAINTNL 0.8599 / 0.8598 / 0.8596, BOMB 0.8444 in all three, LOCATION 0.8292 / 0.8293 / 0.8292; IoU unchanged on every film. Variant 1 moves 53 INTRO frames, 17 LOCATION and 1 BALLOON by more than 0.005; 9 fall by more than 0.005, worst LOCATION 7756 at -0.0154, the rest LOCATION 7723 to 7800 and BALLOON 254 at -0.0076. At LOCATION 7756 (`mise run frame -- --film location --ticks 7756 --var DBG_ACTOR_LIGHT=0,1`) our helicopter does not overlap the capture's exactly: its long rotor blades and tail boom fall where the capture shows terrain and sky, so the body's changed shades are scored partly on pixels where the two already differ. The change darkens the body; whether the darker body matches the capture's could not be judged at this size. Variant 2 falls on 28 frames and gains half as much on INTRO.
- **Verdict**: adopted, variant 1: INTRO rises by 0.0062 and no film falls by more than 0.002. The control `Rᵀ · L` scores below it on INTRO and BALLOON, as the live reading predicts.
- **Lives in**: [`terrain-shade.md`](../../../research/playone-exe/terrain-shade.md) section 4.3; `ShadeLight::for_actor`.

## 2026-11-16: What relighting actors costs a render

- **Question**: what does decoding and uploading each actor's shape per light cost a whole-film render, on the GPU and on lavapipe?
- **Method**: `stuntreel INTRO.FLM --out-dir` timed end to end, RTX 4070 Laptop GPU and `--software-only` (lavapipe), one run each. Before: the sweep's binary with `DBG_ACTOR_LIGHT=0`. Variants of the upload, each timed on lavapipe: (a) a new `GpuModel` per actor and light; (b) one model per actor, its vertex buffer rewritten with `write_buffer` when the decoded vertices change; (c) as (b) with the write skipped, a probe that draws stale colours; (d) the lit vertices appended to the frame's one uniform-buffer write and drawn from that buffer; (e) as (b) with a new mapped-at-creation vertex buffer in place of the write.
- **Result**: before, GPU 6.1 s and lavapipe 12.5 s. (a) GPU 7.8 s, lavapipe 55.0 s. (b) GPU 6.0 s, lavapipe 23.9 s; it rewrites the vertices on 951 of INTRO's 3,823 rendered ticks. (c) lavapipe 13.5 s. (d) GPU 8.4 s, lavapipe 167.8 s. (e) lavapipe 26.0 s. So the decode costs little, and on lavapipe each frame that changes the vertex data of a buffer in flight costs about 11 ms, whether written into the buffer or given a new mapped buffer. Why is not measured.
- **Verdict**: (b) kept: the GPU render time is unchanged and lavapipe takes about twice as long on `INTRO`. The lavapipe cost is left to a follow-up issue.
- **Lives in**: the PR; `SceneRenderer::relight`.

## 2026-11-16: The frames R · L lowers

- **Question**: why do BALLOON 254 and LOCATION 7723 to 7800 fall by more than 0.005 under `R · L`, and what does TRAINTNL, the other film whose mean falls, lose?
- **Method**: `mise run parity` with `DBG_ACTOR_LIGHT` at 0 and at 1, on the GPU, per-frame `ssim` from each film's `report.json`; then `mise run frame -- --film <film> --ticks <tick> --var DBG_ACTOR_LIGHT=0,1` at BALLOON 254, TRAINTNL 1233 and LOCATION 7734, each panel capture, before, after.
- **Result**: BALLOON moves 24 frames by more than 0.0005, mean 0.8174 to 0.8175, worst 254 (-0.0076), best 452 (+0.0031); TRAINTNL 7, mean 0.8599 to 0.8598, worst 1233 (-0.0045); LOCATION 85, mean 0.8292 to 0.8293, worst 7756 (-0.0154), best 452 (+0.0101). At BALLOON 254 the parachutist, seen from behind and pitched, keeps a uniformly dark olive body in the capture; under `R · L` the lower back and legs turn lighter, under the world light they were nearer the capture. At TRAINTNL 1233 the biplane, head on, changes the shade of a few fuselage faces. At LOCATION 7734, as at 7756, our helicopter's rotor and tail boom already miss the capture's, and the body darkens.
- **Verdict**: not explained. The BALLOON fall is on the parachutist's lower body; whether its legs are a sub-model with its own `ROT*` turn, which [`terrain-shade.md`](../../../research/playone-exe/terrain-shade.md) open question 3 leaves open (#271), was not checked. The LOCATION falls sit on a helicopter whose geometry already differs from the capture's. No film's mean falls by more than 0.0002 (TRAINTNL, -0.00015).
- **Lives in**: the PR.
